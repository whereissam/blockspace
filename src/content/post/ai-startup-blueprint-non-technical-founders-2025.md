# Complete Guide: AI Startup Blueprint from Zero to Million Dollars for Non-Technical People in 2025

In the wave of technological innovation, AI is undoubtedly the hottest field at present. However, many people avoid this huge business opportunity because they believe they lack technical background. Successful entrepreneur [Greg Isenberg](https://www.gregisenberg.com/) shared in a recent in-depth conversation how non-technical people can build successful AI businesses in 2025, emphasizing that a lack of technical background is merely a limiting mindset.

Greg himself is a successful entrepreneur who built and sold three companies before turning 30, and is currently running a million-dollar AI company called Boring Marketing. ## The Transition from "Dumb Software" to "Smart Software"

To understand the potential of AI entrepreneurship, we first need to understand the nature of the current technological revolution. Greg describes this revolution as a transition from dumb software to smart software.

Before the emergence of AI, all software was dumb—completely reliant on human input to perform tasks. For example, CRM systems like Salesforce required salespeople to manually add sales leads. This type of dumb software has already created a multi-trillion-dollar industry.

Today, we are entering the era of smart software. AI can inject human-level intelligence into software. This is not just automation, but the creation of systems capable of thinking, analyzing, and executing complex tasks. When we can unlock human-level intelligence and behavior at scale, society will undergo fundamental changes.

Despite AI being widely discussed, Greg believes its impact is still underestimated. For those hearing this information now, they are early participants with the opportunity to create businesses around this transformation.

## Five-Step AI Entrepreneurship Blueprint

Greg shares a five-step framework guiding non-technical people on how to build AI businesses in 2025. This framework is not just theoretical guidance but includes specific methods and tools for practical implementation.

### Step 1: Identify Painful Workflows

The foundation of AI entrepreneurship is finding problems worth solving. Greg observes that whenever we see an export button in SaaS software, there is a potential AI business opportunity worth $1 million to $100 million in annual revenue.

This is because when people export data, it's usually for some kind of analysis. With the development of AI, this analysis can be intelligentized and automated. The key is to focus on workflows that are repetitive and time-consuming.

Most people focus on AI entrepreneurship because they feel unsatisfied with their current jobs and want to unlock more potential. Therefore, the best AI business ideas often come from people in jobs they don't like. They have a deep understanding of workflows and know which parts are most time-consuming and painful. This inside perspective is a goldmine for discovering business opportunities.

In practical terms, Greg suggests examining your daily work, identifying those repetitive, annoying tasks, and then thinking about what it would look like if these tasks could be completed through AI. This is the key question for discovering entrepreneurial opportunities.

A practical case study is Icon, an AI advertising production company. The process of creating Facebook and Instagram ads is typically very tedious, requiring finding actors, managing creators, editing videos, and writing copy. Icon solved this pain point through AI, enabling creative teams to produce ten times the number of ads in the same time period, and these ads don't look AI-generated but are high-quality professional work.

### Step 2: Develop the First Version Quickly

After finding the problem, the next step is to quickly launch a Minimum Viable Product (MVP). Greg prefers to call it a Minimum Lovable Product. The key is not to pursue perfection but to get the product to market as quickly as possible to validate demand.

The mistake many entrepreneurs make is that after finding a pain point, they immediately plan to spend 6-12 months building a fully-featured product. But the goal should not be to build a perfect product, but to create a foundation and then iterate upon it.

In a practical demonstration, Greg showed how to use AI tools like V0.dev to design a professional-level website in just a few minutes. From concept to deploying a new business website actually takes only about 24 hours. In such a short time, entrepreneurs can get something that would have cost hundreds of thousands of dollars a few years ago.

In addition to V0.dev, Greg also demonstrated Manus, an AI agent platform that can help with market research, competitive analysis, and product design. These tools greatly lower the technical barriers to entrepreneurship, allowing non-technical people to quickly start AI businesses.

The key to using these tools is to treat them as conversational partners, not simple input/output systems. For example, after providing initial design requirements to V0.dev, you can continue to give feedback, request more colors, change the logo, or add images. This iterative process is key to achieving a quality product.

Using Manus, Greg was able to analyze the acne patch market and design a competitive product for the adult market. Manus automatically completed tasks such as market research, competitive analysis, product design, and marketing strategy, accomplishing in 12 minutes what would have taken a team days to complete in the past.

### Step 3: Develop Distribution Channels Daily

After having a product, the next challenge is acquiring users. In a world where tools make product development increasingly easy, distribution channels become a key competitive advantage.

When technology makes what once cost hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars to develop cost nearly zero, distribution becomes the real moat. Greg emphasizes the importance of building an audience and proposes the ACP framework: first build an Audience, then create a Community, and finally develop a Product. This is contrary to the traditional method of having a product first and then building an audience.

To systematically build an audience, Greg shares a four-step system:

1. **Identify your target audience**: Understand what they're not getting from others. If you're not getting enough engagement, start from a broader level than your niche.
2. **Choose content formats**: Select one content format for each workday, look for successful formats outside your domain and bring them into your field. Format is the secret to building an audience, just as every successful YouTube channel has its unique format.
3. **Build systems**: Create your "creative faucet" program, find ways to inspire creativity (such as listening to podcasts, reading blogs, etc.), capture ideas, and schedule content. This ensures you always have fresh, interesting content to share.
4. **Set goals**: Set specific goals, such as building a 25,000-person email list or achieving $1 million in sales through social media. Don't just focus on follower count, but consider more downstream goals like revenue or email lists.

Greg emphasizes that many people don't regularly post content or build an audience because they fear judgment from friends and family. Posting a tweet and getting no likes or shares doesn't feel good. Similarly, people want to be proud of the products they release and are unwilling to release something they're not satisfied with. But the path to perfection is actually through consistency and iteration—releasing content, getting feedback, and then improving.

### Step 4: Master Retention and Perfect the Core Loop

Many AI startups attract initial users but fail to retain them. Greg emphasizes the importance of the core loop—the core functionality in the product that drives users to continuously return.

Most AI startups rely on curiosity revenue—users only try these products out of curiosity, but the core value creation doesn't exist. This leads to a high start but low finish, unable to sustain development.

To understand if the core loop is effective, two types of analysis need to be combined:

1. **Behavioral analysis**: Observing data on how users use the product, such as retention rate, daily active users, etc.
2. **Attitudinal analysis/voice of customer**: Directly asking users what they like and don't like.

Focusing only on data while ignoring customer feelings is dangerous. For example, a customer spent 45 minutes at Walmart and spent $200, which from the data seems like a successful shopping experience. But behavioral analysis can't capture the fact that this customer spent more time because they couldn't find what they wanted, and they had actually planned to spend $500.

Advocating for "data informs, not drives"—data should be part of the decision, not the whole. This balance can help entrepreneurs create more differentiated products.

A success story is Calendly. Despite being a relatively simple scheduling tool, by focusing on doing one thing well (scheduling) rather than spreading attention across multiple features, it has built a multi-billion dollar enterprise. Even though Google offers similar functionality, Calendly has built a unique brand and user loyalty by focusing on the core loop.

### Step 5: Partner with Creators to Scale

The final step is leveraging creator partnerships to accelerate growth. While building your own audience is important, partnering with creators who already have an audience can greatly speed up the process.

Consider giving creators 25%, 40%, or 50% of revenue, coming up with innovative ways to collaborate. This doesn't need to be a permanent arrangement but can provide huge momentum while you build your own audience.

Many entrepreneurs hesitate because they don't know how to approach creators. Greg points out that this is just a limiting mindset—even well-known creators are normal people, just receiving more messages than average. The simplest method is to directly send a DM.

An effective approach strategy is to create a 32-second selfie video, briefly introducing yourself and your project, making a specific request (not asking for coverage, but for a conversation), and promising to provide value. Greg personally practiced this method, successfully reaching out to 175 journalists and achieving an extremely high response rate.

This strategy is not only applicable to finding creator collaborations but also for gaining media coverage. Many startup founders asked Greg how he got so much media exposure, and the answer was that he directly contacted journalists himself, rather than through PR firms. By offering value and building relationships, rather than directly asking for coverage, he achieved results far beyond expectations.

## Key Differences Between Success and Failure

When asked about the difference between successful entrepreneurs and those who fail, Greg pointed out two key factors:

1. **Intellectual curiosity**: Successful entrepreneurs have a strong intellectual curiosity about using new products and pushing ideas out into the world. They hear about a new tool and want to try it, constantly exploring and learning.
2. **Willingness to start from zero**: Successful people are willing to get their hands dirty, learn new skills, and embrace a beginner's mindset. They don't consider certain work beneath their level.

Greg shared a story about a famous factory owner who spent one day each year sweeping the factory floor to maintain a connection with the base operations of his business. This humility and willingness to understand every aspect of the business is key to success.

As people gain experience, they often feel ashamed to be beginners or novices. But the key to an entrepreneur's success is precisely the ability to go back to zero and be a beginner. This mindset is crucial for capitalizing on gold rush periods or new waves.

## Future Opportunities: Enormous Business Opportunities in AI

Greg believes that AI will reshape two main areas, providing huge opportunities for entrepreneurs:

1. **SaaS industry**: Through vibes coding (using AI tools to rapidly develop software), entrepreneurs can create cheaper or free versions of existing SaaS products, targeting specific regions or markets. For example, creating a DocuSign alternative for Germany, or a monday.com alternative for Canada, offering similar services at a tenth of the price. Existing large SaaS companies generate billions in annual revenue, so even capturing just 1-2% of the market share can create a significant business.
2. **Consulting/agency business**: Using AI agents to replace part of traditional consulting work, providing lower-cost alternatives. Large consulting firms like McKinsey have thousands of employees, but if entrepreneurs can immediately generate a junior McKinsey analyst, there will be opportunities to provide similar services at a lower cost.

This doesn't mean companies like McKinsey or Adobe will disappear, but that there will be billions of dollars in opportunities waiting for entrepreneurs to provide cheaper alternative services. These opportunities are far from being fully exploited at present, even if some people in the comments section believe these opportunities won't work, or that large companies have brand trust advantages. In fact, there is huge space to provide more cost-effective alternatives.

## Conclusion: Unprecedented Entrepreneurial Opportunities

Today's entrepreneurial environment is unprecedentedly favorable for newcomers. Anyone can register for Instagram, TikTok, or X accounts for free, instantly gaining global distribution channels. There are 6.5 billion people on these platforms, most with credit cards attached. This has never happened in history.

With the rapid development of AI tools, barriers to entrepreneurship have dropped to historic lows. The key to modern entrepreneurship is no longer technical knowledge, but these three elements:

1. Finding an underserved niche market
2. Creating content that resonates with people
3. Using AI tools to quickly launch products and get users to pay

Modern business education should revolve around these three core elements: how to achieve viral spread on social media, how to use AI to implement product ideas that resonate with audiences, and how to develop effective pricing strategies and business models around these products.

For those who have always considered themselves outsiders without technical backgrounds, this is a clear message. The current AI wave offers you an excellent opportunity, and the best time to enter is now. When you encounter pain points at work, don't just complain, but think about how to transform this pain point into a business opportunity. The most painful parts of your daily work might just be the foundation for the next million-dollar AI startup.
