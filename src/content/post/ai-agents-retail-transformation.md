In 2024, dubbed the breakout year for AI Agents, an increasing number of AI startups are focusing on developing intelligent systems that genuinely help individuals and businesses complete tasks. Particularly in the retail industry, AI Agents have begun to penetrate every aspect from marketing to customer service, from physical stores to backend data systems. The co-founder and CEO of Youzan, Leo Huang, shared how AI Agents are transforming the retail industry and enterprise services on the podcast "[What's Next Tech Insider](https://open.spotify.com/episode/3TnpYLx92H0otXahTjs2iI?si=f4QvUz3YQCSjSj8yKmPS8A)."

## The Development Stages of AI Agents

The development of AI Agents has gone through distinct phases, evolving from simple conversational assistants to complex decision-making systems, with continuously improving technical capabilities and application value.

### 1. Copilot Mode: Optimizing Conversational Interaction

The first stage of AI adopts a Copilot mode, primarily leveraging the dialogue and interaction capabilities of large language models. Traditional software requires users to master various functions and build workflows themselves, creating a dilemma where more features lead to poorer usability. Copilot mode fundamentally changes this situation.

Users can express their needs through natural language dialogue, and the system automatically calls upon underlying capabilities to create workflows that solve problems. This approach makes complex functionality easy to use without requiring users to memorize complicated operation steps. It essentially solves the software usability problem, transforming features from obstacles into assets.

This stage has already brought about paradigm optimization and innovation in products, changing the fundamental way people interact with software. Through language as an interface, software becomes more natural and intuitive, significantly lowering the learning threshold.

### 2. Intelligent Analysis and Planning: Realizing the Consultant Role

Second-stage AI not only provides interaction optimization but also possesses thinking, reasoning, and planning capabilities. It can understand users' true underlying needs and provide comprehensive solutions from goals to execution plans.

This is equivalent to AI taking on the role of a consultant, capable of deep analysis and decision support. From business analysis and needs insights to solution formulation and implementation recommendations, AI can provide comprehensive business guidance. At this stage, AI can think more deeply about "why to do this" rather than just "how to do this."

This capability is realized thanks to breakthroughs in the reasoning abilities of large language models. The emergence of tools like DeepSearch marks the gradual maturation of AI applications in the consulting domain. Although there are still controversies about its effectiveness in certain vertical domains, the direction of development is clear.

### 3. Autonomous Decision Systems: Implementing Closed-Loop Workflows

By the third stage, AI has developed into a system capable of making autonomous decisions within certain parameters. From planning formulation to task execution and problem-solving, AI can continuously explore solutions within its own cycle.

This stage realizes true intelligent automation, allowing certain workflows to be completely taken over by AI, with humans only needing to supervise and confirm at key points. Unlike simple automation, AI can handle uncertainty and independently seek solutions when encountering problems during execution.

Although most applications are currently in the transition from the first stage to the second stage, prototypes of the third stage have already begun to appear in certain fields, such as AI tools in the code writing domain. With further technological development, fully autonomous decision-making AI will become a reality in more domains.

## AI Applications in the Retail Industry

In the retail sector, AI Agents have demonstrated practical value in multiple aspects, transforming traditional business operations.

### Revolutionary Improvements in Operational Efficiency

AI has significantly enhanced the speed and accuracy of daily retail operations. In product management, operations such as listing, shipping, and inventory adjustment have been reduced from potentially hours of work to just minutes. This efficiency improvement not only saves time but also reduces human error.

Content production and publishing have also become highly automated. AI can automatically generate product descriptions, marketing copy, and social media content based on product characteristics and target audiences. This allows operations staff to focus on more creative and strategic tasks rather than being bogged down by mundane daily work.

Store data analysis has also become more intelligent. AI can automatically generate performance reports, identify sales trends, and provide specific improvement suggestions. This makes data-driven decision-making no longer the exclusive domain of large enterprises; small merchants can also easily access deep business insights.

### Precise Development of Long-Tail Markets

AI has unlocked the ability to develop new business opportunities, especially in precise marketing for long-tail markets. In traditional retail operations, identifying and managing potential customer groups (such as users who visited the store in the past 30 days but did not make purchases) requires extensive manual operations and analysis. This meant that only merchants of a certain scale could maintain stable operations teams to execute such tasks.

Through AI automation, the system can automatically identify eligible users daily and send marketing pushes, whether it's to one user or a million. This capability makes precise marketing for long-tail markets possible, reaching customer groups that were previously unattainable due to efficiency issues.

When the cost of intelligence is lower than the cost of human labor, things that were not worth doing become feasible, opening up new avenues for retail business expansion. The scale effect of AI makes small-amount, high-frequency marketing activities economically viable, greatly expanding business boundaries.

### Personalization Upgrade in Customer Service

In the customer service domain, AI has also brought revolutionary changes. Intelligent customer service can handle a large volume of standardized inquiries while providing personalized product and service recommendations. Multi-channel customer interaction management has become more coordinated and consistent, enhancing the overall customer experience.

AI can remember each customer's preferences and historical interactions, providing a coherent service experience. Regardless of the channel through which a customer makes contact, AI can identify the customer's identity and provide personalized service, eliminating the information gap issues common in traditional customer service.

Notably, with the decreasing cost of AI, more and more personalized services that were previously only available to high-end customers can now be offered to a broader customer base. This not only improves customer satisfaction but also enhances brand loyalty.

## The Gap Between Technology and Implementation

Despite rapid technological development, market acceptance still lags significantly behind, constituting a major challenge for AI implementation.

### Cultural Barriers to Enterprise Acceptance

Chinese enterprises generally have a lower acceptance level of AI Agents. Most enterprises are not ready to hand over organizational workflows to AI systems and have low trust in new technologies. An interesting phenomenon is that AI applications typically need to be driven by the CEO, while frontline employees often resist, believing that AI cannot handle complex work.

This phenomenon of top-down pushing against bottom-up resistance is quite common in Chinese enterprises, reflecting the cultural challenges in organizational change. Employees may worry about AI replacing their jobs or may not understand the value of new technologies. This situation is relatively milder in overseas enterprises, especially in the United States, where the B2B market is more mature and more receptive to new technologies.

Overcoming this barrier requires more demonstration of successful cases and systematic change management. Enterprise leaders need to clearly position AI as enhancing rather than replacing human labor and create the right incentive mechanisms to promote AI adoption.

### Contradiction Between Technology Iteration and Product Stability

Balancing technology and certainty is another key issue. To increase the certainty of AI decisions, developers often need to reduce its intelligence level, which contradicts the rapidly evolving model capabilities.

Each time a fundamental model is upgraded (such as the recent release of GPT-4o), previous development work done to ensure stability may need to be readjusted. This tug-of-war between technology iteration and product stability forces development teams to continuously reassess product architecture.

This situation is similar to "chasing a moving target," and AI application developers need to find the optimal balance between product stability and technological advancement. Product design should consider the direction of future technological development and leave flexibility to adapt to improvements in model capabilities.

### Evolution Challenges in Business Models

The business model for AI services is evolving from traditional subscription models to usage-based billing, and ultimately toward outcome-based payment. This reflects the evolution of AI products from tools to services to result delivery.

With the improvement of AI capabilities, the "pay for results" model will become mainstream because it better aligns with enterprise customer expectations and the essence of AI problem-solving. However, implementing this model requires solving several key challenges: how to define and measure "results," how to promise specific results when uncertainty still exists, and how to develop appropriate pricing strategies for enterprises of different scales.

A unique challenge in the Chinese market is the lower willingness to pay. Compared to overseas markets, Chinese enterprises traditionally have a lower acceptance of payment for SaaS services. This requires AI service providers to demonstrate clearer ROI and may need more innovative business models to adapt to market characteristics.

## Data Integration and Infrastructure

In the retail industry, data silos have always been a major obstacle to achieving comprehensive digitalization and AI application. Solving this problem requires approaches from both technical architecture and organizational processes.

### The Root and Impact of Data Silos

The historical systems and data architectures accumulated during enterprise digitalization processes are often fragmented and operate independently. Different departments use different systems, resulting in data that cannot flow and integrate.

In the retail industry, this problem is particularly prominent. From inventory management and order processing to customer relationship management, data from various links is locked in different systems. When enterprises attempt to apply AI to business processes, this lack of data interoperability becomes a major obstacle.

Data silos not only limit the capabilities of AI but also cause inefficient decision-making and inconsistent user experiences. For example, when online and offline customer data cannot be integrated, it becomes impossible to provide a unified omnichannel shopping experience.

### Building a Unified Data Platform

The solution is to build a unified data and business middle platform as the infrastructure for AI applications. Through this platform, data from different departments and channels can be uniformly managed and called upon, providing AI with a comprehensive business perspective.

Specifically, this platform includes several core components: a unified customer profile platform that integrates data from all customer touchpoints; a product and inventory management system that provides real-time inventory and sales data; and a transaction processing system that handles order information from all channels.

This integration not only breaks down data silos but also enables AI to provide coherent services and decision support across broader business scenarios. The data platform is not just a technical tool but a new way of business operation that requires corresponding adjustments in organizational culture and processes.

### API-ization and Microservice Architecture

To ensure data fluidity and system flexibility, an API-ization and microservice architecture approach is recommended. This architecture allows different systems to communicate through standard interfaces, enabling data to flow freely.

Under this architecture, new AI functionalities can be added to the system as independent services without changing the overall architecture. This greatly accelerates the development and deployment speed of AI applications, enabling enterprises to respond quickly to market changes and technological advances.

Successful AI transformation requires enterprises to rethink their data architecture from a strategic level, not just adding AI functionalities to existing systems. A truly AI-driven enterprise needs data flow and integration to operate as efficiently as the enterprise's nervous system.

## Future Development Trends

Looking ahead, the outlook for AI Agents in retail and enterprise services is optimistic, with several forward-looking development directions worth noting.

### The Rise of Vertical Domain Solutions

In the coming year, a batch of vertical domain solutions based on Multi-Agent architecture will emerge. These solutions will be able to provide end-to-end services for specific industry scenarios, forming closed loops from problem analysis to solution execution and result evaluation.

The retail industry is likely to be one of the first to benefit, as its data is rich and its business models are relatively standardized. For example, an AI system specifically designed for clothing retail can integrate supply chain management, inventory optimization, product recommendations, and customer service functionalities to provide a complete industry solution.

The characteristics of these vertical solutions lie in their deep understanding of industry knowledge, not just the application of general AI capabilities. They can understand industry-specific terminology, processes, and best practices, thereby providing more precise decision support.

### AI Empowerment and Reshaping of Professional Roles

In professional roles, AI Agents will achieve performance comparable to human professionals in multiple vertical professional domains. Particularly in content creation, market analysis, customer service, and other fields, AI will be able to independently complete most tasks and collaborate with humans at key points.

This does not mean the disappearance of human roles but rather their redefinition. Humans will take on more strategic guidance and creative thinking roles, while AI handles execution and optimization. For example, marketers can focus on brand strategy and creative direction, leaving content production, channel placement, and data analysis to AI.

This human-machine collaboration model will become the standard for future work methods, requiring people to develop new skills and thinking patterns. Professionals who can effectively guide and utilize AI will gain new competitive advantages in the workplace.

### Innovative Models of AI-Native Enterprises

A more revolutionary prediction is the rise of AI-native enterprises. A batch of entirely new business models will emerge that rely on extremely few personnel and extensive AI support. Particularly in the consumer goods sector, we may see brands operated by one person or just a few people.

These enterprises handle the entire process from product design to market analysis to customer service through AI, achieving extremely high operational efficiency. Work that traditionally required dozens of people may only need a few people plus powerful AI systems to complete.

This will significantly lower the barriers to entrepreneurship, allowing more innovative ideas to be rapidly commercialized. At the same time, it will also prompt existing enterprises to rethink their organizational structures and staffing configurations, moving towards leaner and more agile directions.

### Popularization of Personalized Experiences

In terms of consumer experience, AI will make highly personalized products and services commonplace. Personalized production and services that were traditionally limited by cost and efficiency will achieve mass customization through AI assistance.

This includes not only personalized marketing content and recommendations but will also extend to the personalized design and production of the products themselves. AI-designed personalized clothing or accessories have already begun to appear, and this trend will become more widespread in the future.

In the service domain, AI will be able to provide personalized consultation and recommendations based on consumers' individual situations. For example, in health and nutrition, AI can provide customized health advice and product recommendations based on an individual's physical condition, lifestyle habits, and preferences.

## Paradigm Shift and Industry Restructuring

Summarizing the impact of AI Agents on the retail industry: This is not the end of traditional SaaS models but a profound paradigm shift and upgraded iteration.

### Continuity and Evolution

AI progress is built on a foundation of digitalization. Those enterprises that have accumulated rich data and knowledge in the digital era will have more opportunities to unleash maximum value in the AI era. This continuity indicates that technological evolution is gradual rather than disruptive, even though the pace of change is accelerating.

If enterprises have not achieved digitalization, they will find it difficult to effectively utilize AI. Similarly, if enterprises have not accumulated knowledge and data, the effectiveness of AI will be greatly reduced. Therefore, efforts invested in digital transformation in the past will not be wasted but will become important assets in the AI era.

This perspective provides enterprises with a clear path: they need not worry about past investments being completely disrupted by new technologies but should instead consider how to integrate AI capabilities based on existing foundations to achieve smooth transitions and maximize value.

### Diverse Coexistence in the Business Landscape

The future business landscape will be characterized by diverse coexistence, with both traditional software enterprises achieving upgraded innovation through the integration of AI capabilities and AI-native companies built on entirely new concepts. This diversity will drive innovation and progress across the entire industry.

Different types of enterprises have their own advantages and disadvantages. Traditional enterprises possess rich industry experience and customer bases but may have inertia in technological innovation. Emerging AI-native enterprises are technologically advanced but may lack deep industry understanding. The most successful enterprises of the future may be those that can combine industry insights with technological innovation.

This diverse competitive environment will ultimately promote the evolution of the entire retail ecosystem, creating more value for consumers. The coexistence and competition of different models will accelerate the pace of industry innovation, pushing the retail industry towards a more intelligent and humanized future.

### Shift in Thinking from Tools to Results

The most core transformation in the AI era is the shift from tool-oriented thinking to result-oriented thinking. Enterprises no longer merely purchase and use tools but seek measurable business outcomes. The value of AI systems lies not in their technical complexity but in the actual value they can create for enterprises and consumers.

In the traditional SaaS model, enterprises purchased functionality and licenses, and users needed to learn how to use these tools to complete work. In the AI era, enterprises will directly purchase results—increased sales, reduced customer service costs, enhanced customer loyalty, and so on.

This transformation affects not only product forms but also redefines pricing models and customer relationships. When AI service providers promise and deliver specific business results, the relationship between enterprises and suppliers evolves from purely transactional to a deeper partnership.

In this sense, AI Agents represent a fundamental transformation of business models and value delivery methods, redefining the relationship between enterprises and technology, as well as the interaction modes between enterprises and consumers.

## Conclusion

The application of AI Agents in the retail industry is moving from concept to reality, upgrading from tools to decision systems. While this process faces challenges in technology, culture, and business models, the direction of development is clear.

This paradigm shift, though challenging, also harbors enormous opportunities. Those enterprises that can adapt early and embrace this transformation will occupy advantageous positions in future retail and service domains, leading the industry towards more intelligent, efficient, and human-centered directions.

Ultimately, the value of AI Agents lies not in the technology itself but in how it changes the way people work and consume, how it creates new business opportunities, and how it brings broader value to society. In this sense, what we are experiencing is not merely a technological succession but a profound business and social transformation.
