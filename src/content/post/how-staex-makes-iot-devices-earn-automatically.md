## First Stop: Who is Staex? Innovators Rising from a Telecom Corner

Founder Alexandra Mikityuk, along with a group of tech enthusiasts, wanted to solve a major problem: the increasing number of IoT devices (like surveillance cameras, smart meters, EV charging stations) are becoming as crowded and chaotic as Taipei MRT during peak hours. ### "Tunnel" Network: Faster, Cheaper, and More Secure Enterprise Connectivity

In the digital age, a business's IoT devices are like people flowing through a city, constantly exchanging data, but keeping these devices smoothly and securely interconnected isn't easy. Imagine running a chain of braised food shops with 50 branches across Taiwan, each with surveillance cameras, electronic cash registers, and even IoT devices. Managing connections of this scale using traditional VPN methods is not only slow but also requires a professional technical team for maintenance, making it quite costly.

Staex's "tunnel" technology completely overturns this model. This technology ensures secure connections between all devices through encrypted channels, allowing businesses to abandon VPN hassles, not only increasing connection speed by 70% but also reducing traffic costs by 70%. Actual data shows that after adopting Staex, businesses can save about €1.7 (approximately NT$60) in communication fees per device per month. For businesses with tens of thousands of devices, this means saving over NT$700,000 annually – not a small amount!

Beyond cost savings, Staex's "tunnel" technology has also put considerable effort into security. Traditional networks are vulnerable to hacker attacks, especially when one device is compromised, potentially endangering the entire system. But Staex employs a "zero-trust network" architecture, where each device has its own independent public-private key pair, like a digital ID card. Devices without verification simply cannot enter the system. Even if a device is hacked, the attack cannot spread to other devices because Staex's "tunnel isolation" technology immediately disconnects the affected device, ensuring the entire network remains uncompromised.

In terms of performance, Staex uses the Dijkstra algorithm to automatically find the optimal transmission path in complex network environments. The system supports 12,000 nodes operating simultaneously and can be activated in just minutes, meaning businesses can expand their networks anytime without worrying about speed and stability issues.

More importantly, Staex's "tunnel" technology can be directly applied to any device supporting IP protocols, allowing businesses to upgrade to the latest security standards without replacing existing equipment. For example, an ordinary IP camera, through Staex's technology, can become a highly encrypted remote monitoring device, enabling businesses to conduct data analysis more securely and even develop new business models.

This technology also complies with the EU's latest network security standards (NIS2 and Cyber Resilience Act), meaning businesses can not only enhance security but also meet international norms, reducing future regulatory risks. In the wave of digital transformation, what Staex provides is not just a connectivity solution, but a key technology that can help businesses develop in the long term.

Simply put, Staex doesn't just help businesses "save money"; it actually helps them solve real problems, enhance competitiveness, and even create new revenue sources. This is where Staex's true value lies.

## Sixth Stop: Staex's Tokenomics - Function and Value of SPNX

After discussing so much about technology and cases, one core aspect of Staex's business model is the SPNX token. SPNX is a utility token playing a key role in the Staex ecosystem. The Tokenomics detailed in technical documents provides the economic foundation for Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks (DePIN).

SPNX's main functions include purchasing network certificates (provided as NFTs), which allow IoT devices to connect to Staex's root nodes for secure data transmission. If users wish to run nodes and participate in network maintenance, they can stake SPNX tokens to receive rewards. Root node operators earn SPNX income by selling certificates. The minimum usage period for certificates is one month, but if unsatisfied with the service, users can revoke certificates early, paying only for the period used. This ensures root nodes provide high-quality service and incentivizes operators to maintain network stability.

SPNX also introduces a "non-binary trust" mechanism, where trust levels are linked to the amount of SPNX held by nodes. Root nodes holding more tokens can set higher certificate prices, incentivizing operators to improve service quality and maintain network stability. For device owners, the SPNX network offers high-security advantages: hiding real IP addresses, defending against DDoS attacks, implementing end-to-end encryption, all at a cost lower than traditional cloud solutions – especially critical for IoT and DePIN applications. For root node operators, selling certificates and token appreciation bring dual income. In the long term, Staex plans to enable machine-to-machine payments through SPNX, such as drones automatically paying charging fees or charging stations settling directly with power suppliers. This will greatly expand its business potential, especially creating new revenue streams in the DePIN market.

Of the total SPNX supply, 55% is allocated for Public Sale, 15% for Treasury, 12.5% for the Team, 10% for Investors, 5% for Partners & Advisors, and 25% for Community & Network. This distribution ensures balanced interests among all parties, promoting long-term ecosystem development.
