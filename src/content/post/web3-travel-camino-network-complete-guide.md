## What is Camino Network?

Camino Network is like the result of an experienced travel consultant and a blockchain engineer joining forces, bringing personalized travel experiences to travelers through seamless data exchange.

Its core goal is to solve the deeply rooted fragmentation problem in the travel industry, replacing thousands of incompatible APIs and outdated systems with a unified standardized communication layer. This process is not just a technical upgrade, but a reshaping of the economic structure of a trillion-dollar industry—a structure that has long needed renovation.

The travel industry is quite special in many ways.

From an overall perspective, it's a behemoth. In 2023, the travel industry generated over $1.9 trillion in revenue, accounting for nearly 10% of global GDP. But its technological foundation is like a messy patchwork, leaking money everywhere.

Hotels have to pay high commissions to Online Travel Agencies (OTAs like Booking.com or Expedia). It's estimated that the US hotel industry alone pays out $4-8 billion annually in transaction fees and commissions just for OTA bookings, with commission rates typically ranging from 10% to 30% of booking amounts.

Airlines face the same situation. To sell tickets to us, they rely on platforms called "Global Distribution Systems" (GDS), like Amadeus, to get tickets to travel agencies or booking websites. They have to pay fees to these platforms for each ticket sold on each flight segment.

Some companies spend millions of dollars maintaining connections between a bunch of systems to keep everything running smoothly.

The reasons for this technological chaos are as numerous as stars in the sky, all simmering in a giant pot of inefficiency. Legacy systems from the 80s and 90s were never completely phased out; data often flows in one direction and becomes outdated before it can be used; hotels, airlines, and car rental services operate independently, using different systems and communication methods; payment settlements between partners can drag on for weeks or even months. Then the pandemic came and exposed the fragility of the entire system. When the dust settled, companies that survived were left with razor-thin margins and barely any money to invest in innovation. Enough is enough!

## Understanding Camino Network

There are several different perspectives for understanding Camino Network. They're interconnected but worth analyzing separately before integrating them.

First, from an industry perspective: Camino is a tool that helps the travel industry reduce distribution costs, simplify payment settlements, improve data transparency, and ultimately improve unit economics.

Second, from a technical perspective: Camino is a Layer 1 blockchain specifically designed for the travel industry, providing faster, cheaper, and more environmentally friendly transaction processing than traditional blockchains.

Third, from an ecosystem perspective: Camino is an open network collectively owned and governed by travel enterprises, returning ownership to industry participants rather than tech giants or investors.

Fourth, from a community-driven perspective: Camino serves as a platform collectively owned and governed by a validator consortium, enabling industry participants to jointly decide technical standards and network development direction.

To truly understand Camino's potential, we first need to understand the current state of the travel industry.

## The Truth About Travel: We Want Magic, But It's All Reality Behind the Scenes

The travel industry has always created special expectations. You and I both hope that every time we walk into an airport or hotel, we're greeted with thoughtful service, warm smiles, and an unforgettable journey.

We don't want travel to be affected by "trade-offs" and "compromises" like other commercial activities. After all, travel is a way to relax, celebrate, and heal ourselves—it's one of life's small certainties.

But the reality is, the travel industry is a business. And it's a very difficult business to run. Its business model directly affects the level of service it can provide. Here are some major pain points currently facing the travel industry:

- **High Intermediary Fees**: Airlines spend over $7 billion annually on intermediary platforms called GDS (Global Distribution Systems). For every ticket booking, airlines pay a fee. These costs squeeze their profits and make it difficult to lower ticket prices.
- **Fragmented Technology**: A travel agency might need to interface with over 100 different API systems, with large enterprises reaching up to 700! Each system is different, making maintenance extremely troublesome and increasing development costs.
- **Inventory Management Challenges**: Too many empty hotel rooms? Unsold flight seats? These are actually due to inaccurate forecasting, leading to revenue loss. But accurately predicting demand is inherently difficult.
**Payment and Settlement Challenges**: Travel often involves cross-border transactions, resulting in settlements that take 3-5 days with 2-3% handling fees. For the travel industry with already thin margins, this expense creates significant pressure.

### Amadeus's Success Formula: Scale + Technology

Companies like Amadeus are among the few success stories. Founded in the 1980s as a joint venture by several major airlines, it's now one of the world's largest travel technology suppliers.

Their secret to success: building a centralized platform that everyone has to use. If you're a travel agency or airline, you almost have to connect to the Amadeus system to do business.

But here's the problem: if this is so effective, why haven't more companies followed suit? Because building such a platform requires massive investment and user scale. Most small companies simply can't afford it.

Amadeus succeeded because they're big enough and wealthy enough to continuously invest in technology, building stable and reliable systems. This is the power of "economies of scale."

### Past vs. Present: Travel Industry Transformation

In the past, travel agencies didn't have many choices—they used whatever systems were available. That was the era of "suppliers calling the shots."

But now it's different. Consumers have hundreds of choices: various OTA platforms, community reviews, price comparison websites... allowing everyone to choose services that best meet their needs. This means user-friendly technology platforms can better retain customers.

Now it's the era of "demand-driven" markets. Whoever can provide good experiences and be memorable to travelers wins the market.

### The Truth: Good Experience = Spending Money, Independent Companies Can't Bear It

But the problem is also harsh: to create good experiences, you need to invest in technology, train staff, and improve services—all of which cost money. But travel tech companies' average profit margins have dropped from the traditional 20% to just 4%, sometimes even lower.

Companies often face dilemmas:

- Should we cut technology budgets?
- Should we reduce customer service staff?
- Should we move to cheaper locations?
- When training employees, how long can the company survive?

These real problems put pressure on many small and medium-sized companies, making them struggle to breathe.

### One Solution: Consolidation

So we see consolidation trends emerging in the industry:

- GDS systems are becoming increasingly concentrated, with three major players controlling 78% of global ticket distribution
- Travel agency backend mergers and OTA frontend monopolization are becoming increasingly obvious

But this also raises concerns: will the future of travel only be dominated by a few tech giants? Will all experiences become increasingly similar and "standardized"?

We want exactly the opposite. Thousands of thriving independent travel tech companies and service providers, each offering something unique, each providing magical personalized experiences for customers. ## How Camino Network Works

Camino Network was founded in 2021 by Chain4Travel and is a public permissioned blockchain that is both open and has certain access restrictions. Anyone can use the network, but validator nodes need to pass KYC/KYB verification to ensure they are legitimate travel businesses.

Camino consists of three main blockchains:

- **Platform Chain (P-Chain)**: Like the system's "brain," responsible for arranging who maintains the network and ensuring everything runs smoothly.
- **Exchange Chain (X-Chain)**: Like a fast checkout counter, specifically handling simple payments or transfers, particularly suitable for small transactions that need quick completion during travel.
- **Contract Chain (C-Chain)**: This part can run smart programs (like automatic booking or chat accounts) and is compatible with Ethereum, providing powerful functionality.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe1141073-61ef-460b-af82-fe05ea23943c_1024x636.png)

This three-chain architecture enables Camino Network to simultaneously provide performance, flexibility, and compatibility with the Ethereum ecosystem. Assets can be transferred between the three chains through atomic swaps, allowing users to move assets as needed.

### PoSA Consensus Mechanism

Camino Network employs a consensus mechanism called "PoSA," combining the advantages of two technologies: Proof of Stake (PoS) and Proof of Authority (PoA).

**Proof of Stake (PoS)**: Validator nodes need to stake 100,000 CAM tokens, ensuring they have actual economic investment in the network, reducing incentives for malicious behavior.

**Proof of Authority (PoA)**: Validator nodes must be identity-verified legitimate travel companies, adding transparency and credibility to the entire network.

#### Why This Design?

This design gives Camino Network the following advantages:

- ✅ **Fast Transaction Confirmation**: Transactions are completed almost instantly without long waits
- ✅ **High Transaction Processing**: Can handle up to 4,500 transactions per second, making the system super smooth
- ✅ **Energy Efficient and Environmentally Friendly**: Consumes 99.9% less energy than Bitcoin's high-energy mining approach
- ✅ **More Secure**: Effectively prevents fake account abuse (Sybil), hacker attacks, and internal collusion

Overall, PoSA makes Camino Network faster, more stable, and more suitable for enterprise use, especially for industries like travel that require high efficiency and trustworthiness.

### Permissioned Public Blockchain: KYC/KYB Verification

Camino Network is a system called "permissioned public blockchain," which simply means:

**Public**: Anyone can use this blockchain, such as querying data or using applications

**Permissioned**: But if you want to play an important role, such as becoming a transaction validator or developing blockchain applications, you must first pass identity verification

#### Why Identity Verification?

Validator nodes help with "bookkeeping" and maintaining network security. Camino requires these nodes to be legitimate travel companies verified through KYC (Know Your Customer) or KYB (Know Your Business).

This identity verification is managed by the Camino consortium, with the purpose of:

- ✅ Ensuring nodes are trustworthy companies
- ✅ Preventing anonymous, unknown individuals from abusing the system
- ✅ Improving overall network transparency and security

This design is particularly suitable for industries requiring trust and compliance, like travel.

## Camino Messenger

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F601d5328-3c2a-4e43-8324-600ca0458a27_1121x458.png)

Camino's most compelling product is Camino Messenger, a decentralized communication protocol designed specifically for the travel industry built on Camino Network, launched in early 2024. Think of it as WhatsApp, but instead of person-to-person communication, it's server-to-server connections.

Camino Messenger solves this problem in four ways:

1. **Standardized Communication**: Uses standardized communication formats to exchange data (search, booking, payment, etc.)
2. **Direct Connections**: Direct connections without intermediaries
3. **Microfee Structure**: Sets and collects small fees for searches and transactions, charging a small network fee per message (0.00005 EURe), with 70% allocated to validator nodes operating mail servers and 30% for continued development
4. **Micropayment Capability**: Allows suppliers to set small fees for each search request (like 0.0001 EURe), converting search traffic into revenue sources

This solves the industry's long-standing "Look-to-Book (LTB)" problem. The LTB ratio refers to the number of searches needed to generate one actual booking. In some travel verticals, this ratio can be as high as 25,000:1, meaning suppliers must process thousands of search requests to get one actual booking. Processing these search requests requires enormous computational resources and infrastructure, creating a significant cost burden for suppliers.

For example, Lufthansa is using Camino Messenger to distribute flight content. Previously, OTAs needed to access Lufthansa's inventory through GDS or establish expensive direct connections. Now they can connect directly through Messenger, reducing costs for both parties.

## Camino Network's Business Model

For a platform aimed at reducing costs and improving efficiency, it's important to understand how Camino Network achieves its own sustainability.

Camino's business model has three main parts:

1. **Transaction Fees**: All network transactions pay small amounts of CAM tokens as fees. 70% of these fees are allocated to validator nodes, 20% to Chain4Travel for continued development, and 10% are burned.
2. **Camino Messenger Fees**: Each message charges a 0.00005 EURe network fee, with 70% allocated to nodes running message servers and 30% for development.
3. **Service Fee Sharing**: Suppliers can set service fees for their messages (e.g., search responses). Camino takes 30% of these fees as platform fees.

Unlike typical intermediaries, Camino doesn't pursue extracting 15-25% fees from transactions. Instead, it aims to reduce total transaction costs to 3-5% while creating sufficient incentives for network participants (especially validator nodes).

More importantly, as the network gradually decentralizes, most revenue will flow to validator nodes and service providers rather than Chain4Travel. According to the whitepaper, the long-term goal is for industry participants to own and control about half of the network.

## How Blockchain Solves Travel Industry Problems

Why use blockchain to solve these problems? Why not use more traditional technical approaches?

These are reasonable questions, especially considering the hype surrounding cryptocurrency and blockchain. But after deep research, I found blockchain technology particularly suitable for several aspects of the travel industry:

### 1. Disintermediation

Travel industry distribution channels are controlled by a few large intermediaries that charge high fees. Blockchain allows peer-to-peer transactions, eliminating middlemen and significantly reducing costs.

For example, Camino's transaction fees are less than 1%, while traditional payment processors charge 3-4% and GDS charges up to 25%.

### 2. Trust and Transparency

The travel industry involves transactions between many different participants who often lack mutual trust. Blockchain provides an immutable record that all participants can trust.

For example, when flights are delayed, insurance payouts can be automatically processed through smart contracts without lengthy claims processes.

### 3. Micropayment Capability

Blockchain makes micropayments possible that are not viable in traditional payment systems due to high transaction costs. This opens doors to new business models.

For example, through Camino Messenger, suppliers can charge small fees for each search (like 0.0001 EURe), converting search traffic from cost to revenue.

### 4. Industry Ownership

Perhaps most importantly, as a permissioned public blockchain, Camino allows the travel industry to collectively own and manage its digital infrastructure rather than relying on tech giants or external investors.

For an industry long exploited by intermediaries, this is particularly important, returning control and reducing dependencies.

## Challenges and Risks

While prospects look good, Camino Network faces several significant challenges:

### 1. Network Effects and Chicken-Egg Problem

Any network faces a chicken-and-egg problem before reaching critical mass. Suppliers don't want to join networks without enough distributors, and distributors don't want to join networks without enough suppliers.

Camino addresses this challenge by focusing on building a highly relevant, verified core network of participants rather than pursuing pure quantity. Currently, there are over 200 partners and more than 100 validator nodes.

### 2. Technical Complexity

Blockchain remains unfamiliar to many travel enterprises, potentially creating adoption barriers.

Camino addresses this challenge by building user-friendly tools like Camino Messenger that abstract underlying blockchain complexity. Businesses don't need to understand how blockchain works to benefit from it.

### 3. Regulatory Uncertainty

The cryptocurrency and blockchain regulatory environment is still evolving and may present challenges.

Camino actively addresses this challenge by implementing KYC/KYB requirements and working with regulators. As a project focused on utility rather than speculation, it's positioned to face relatively low regulatory risks.

### 4. Vested Interest Opposition

Existing intermediaries may resist innovations threatening their business models.

Camino addresses this challenge by inviting traditional participants to join the network and become validator nodes, making them part of the ecosystem rather than opponents.
