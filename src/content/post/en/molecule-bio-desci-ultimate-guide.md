# European Web3 Exploration | Episode 6: Molecule x Bio - The Ultimate DeSci Guide

Have you ever spent countless hours researching new drug structures, only to discover that patent applications cost tens of thousands of dollars and take years to process? Or perhaps your research findings were rejected by journals and ended up forgotten in a drawer? This isn't just one scientist's personal struggle—it's a pain point of the entire scientific research ecosystem.

## The "Dark Universe" of Science: How Bad Is the Current State?

Every year, global investment in scientific research exceeds $2 trillion, but these astronomical amounts mostly flow to a few well-known institutions and established research directions. Countless valuable research projects are interrupted due to lack of funding, and innovative perspectives are ignored because they don't conform to the mainstream.

The scientific community seems stuck in last century's model. Researchers spend up to 40% of their time applying for funding rather than conducting actual research; knowledge is locked behind expensive academic journal paywalls; patent application costs and time make independent researchers shy away; scientists rarely receive direct economic returns from their research results. While large institutions monopolize resources, opportunities for emerging researchers become increasingly limited.

These long-standing problems have given birth to the Decentralized Science (DeSci) movement, and Molecule is one of the most promising decentralized science platforms I've seen that truly solves funding and intellectual property challenges in scientific research. Since 2021, many research teams have begun obtaining funding and protecting their intellectual property through it.

## Core Concepts: Understanding the Foundation of the DeSci Revolution

Before diving into the world of DeSci, we need to understand several basic concepts. While these concepts may seem complex, they actually reflect a completely new way of organizing and financing scientific research.

### Decentralized Science (DeSci): The Blockchain Revolution in Research

Decentralized Science (DeSci) is a movement that uses blockchain and Web3 technologies to reshape how scientific research is conducted. It aims to create a more open, transparent, fair, and efficient scientific ecosystem, solving problems in traditional scientific systems such as uneven funding distribution, knowledge monopolization, and difficulty in obtaining fair returns for research results.

In traditional scientific models, research funding mainly comes from government agencies, large foundations, or commercial enterprises, with highly centralized decision-making power. DeSci provides scientists with entirely new funding sources and innovation incentive mechanisms by establishing Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs) and using cryptocurrencies and Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs).

## Paul Kohlhaas: The Extraordinary Journey from Biohacker to DeSci Pioneer

The story of Molecule cannot be separated from the unique experiences of its founder, Paul Kohlhaas. This scientific revolutionary's journey began in teenage biohacker forums. If pharmaceutical R&D could be personified, it might be like a composite of Paul—curious, open, unafraid to challenge the status quo, and always focused on the ultimate beneficiaries: patients.

### From Teenage Biohacker to Cryptocurrency Enthusiast

Paul's story began in his teenage years. At 16-18 years old, he was immersed in online biohacker forums, many of which branched off from psychoactive drug harm reduction websites like Erowid. In these communities, he was exposed to the concept of open-source drug research promoted by Alexander Shulgin, the original inventor of MDMA. This experience sparked Paul's deep interest in the concept of open-source drug discovery and development.

As he became more deeply involved in these communities, Paul discovered more compelling phenomena: diabetic patient communities were developing their own open-source insulin synthesis methods, and cancer patients unable to afford drugs costing up to $150,000 were seeking their own alternatives. These communities often obtained basic chemicals from Chinese and Korean chemical manufacturers—as long as these substances weren't patent-protected or listed as controlled substances, manufacturers typically didn't question their intended use.

Simultaneously, Paul began paying attention to macroeconomic issues in the American pharmaceutical system. In the early stages of the opioid crisis becoming apparent, he realized there were fundamental flaws in the pharmaceutical system. On one hand, people were forced to engage in dangerous behavior because they couldn't access affordable medications; on the other hand, the major pharmaceutical system failed to provide patient-centered, affordable healthcare services. His own family's experience being prescribed early ADHD medications and antidepressants further convinced him that this system was not truly serving patient needs.

### Economic and Blockchain-Inspired Turning Point

With these questions in mind, Paul went to the University of St. Gallen in Switzerland to study economics. During his studies, he began trading biotech stocks and discovered that biotech stocks typically traded solely based on their asset value and company burn rate, with price movements showing steady decline until positive data emerged (causing 5-10x jumps) or negative data appeared (causing 80-90% crashes). This extreme volatility made him recognize fundamental efficiency problems in the market.

In mid-2013, a friend interning at Bitcoin Suisse introduced him to Bitcoin, which quickly sent him down the cryptocurrency rabbit hole. He taught himself programming and developed a strong interest in the flourishing open-source software and cryptocurrency communities.

After completing his economics studies, Paul went to South Africa in mid-2015. After briefly working in private equity, he founded his first company, Linium Labs, at age 23, beginning to build early applications on Ethereum and provide blockchain training services to companies. During his time at Linium Labs, he became good friends with Simon de la Rouviere, a pioneer of bonding curves, and developed a strong interest in this asset pricing and information discovery mechanism.

### The Birth of Molecule: A Key Epiphany

While working at Consensys, Paul focused on areas such as data certification, digital identity, and data markets. It was during this work period, from late 2017 to early 2018, that he experienced a key epiphany moment: "What if instead of attaching pictures of cats to NFTs, we attached material composition patents describing the basic intellectual property of new drugs or new therapies?"

This idea became the core concept of Molecule, a platform that would completely change how drug development is conducted. After finishing his work at Consensys, Paul began working full-time on building Molecule, combining his rich experience in both biotechnology and blockchain fields.

### Solving Fundamental Problems in the Pharmaceutical Industry

Paul's core motivation for founding Molecule was to solve fundamental problems in modern drug development:

1. **The Dilemma of Open-Source Drug Development** - When researchers publicly publish new drug targets on platforms like GitHub, these discoveries immediately become unpatentable. Without patent protection, almost no organization is willing to bear the high costs of clinical trials, even for drugs with the potential to cure diseases like malaria that affect hundreds of millions of people.
2. **The Extremely High Cost of Drug Development** - Due to bureaucratized regulatory procedures that haven't changed substantially in decades, drug development costs are enormous, effectively creating a monopoly for large pharmaceutical companies as only they can afford these costs.
3. **Disenfranchisement of Patients and Researchers** - In traditional models, people who actually do the research work and patients who ultimately use the drugs have almost no voice and cannot share in value creation.

Through Molecule's IP-NFT and BioDAO models, Paul is creating a more democratic, transparent, and efficient drug development ecosystem. His vision is that within the next 5-10 years, a drug developed through this decentralized process will be able to benefit patients at lower cost, completely changing how people receive healthcare.

As Paul said: "If our legal system changes, we can treat drug development like we treat software." Just as the open-source software revolution changed the software industry, Paul believes his IP-NFT can be the key to unlocking a pharmaceutical revolution.

## Molecule: A Web3 Platform for Scientific Intellectual Property

Molecule is a groundbreaking decentralized biotech protocol founded in 2018, jointly led by Paul Kohlhaas and Tyler Golato. Its goal is to establish a completely new Web3 marketplace for research-related intellectual property (IP).

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe897471f-19c5-4470-8503-c3d719d4a00b_368x235.png)

Imagine Molecule as a combination of a scientist's digital patent office + funding platform. Scientists can register research projects here, convert research results into digital assets (IP-NFTs), and then find investors willing to fund research.

Unlike traditional biotech companies that focus on developing specific drugs, Molecule is dedicated to creating complete infrastructure that enables any researcher to tokenize their research results, obtain funding, and retain intellectual property control.

### How Does Molecule Work?

Molecule builds a multi-layered ecosystem marketplace that connects researchers and funding sources through innovative mechanisms. Let's dive deep into its core layers:

#### Discovery Layer: Research Project Showcase and Price Discovery

The discovery layer is the entry point to the Molecule platform, where researchers and institutions can showcase their early research and IP. The system uses bonding curve mechanisms for asset pricing and discovery—an innovative financial tool borrowed from the cryptocurrency field.

Paul describes it as a "meme market for therapeutic drugs," allowing patients, researchers, and investors to collectively participate in the price discovery process. This mechanism effectively solves the problem of difficult valuation for early scientific research projects, finding reasonable pricing for research results through collective wisdom.

#### Funding Layer: Diversified Funding Acquisition Channels

Molecule provides multiple funding models to suit the needs of different research projects:

- **Direct IP-NFT Purchase**: Investors can directly purchase complete or partial IP-NFT ownership
- **Research Sponsorship**: Investors can sponsor specific research phases and gain partial ownership
- **Staged Funding**: Funds are released in stages based on research milestones, reducing investment risk
- **Collaborative Incentives**: The system supports innovative collaborative funding mechanisms, such as incentive structures like "anyone who can complete mouse studies gets 2% of the asset"

#### Collaboration Layer: Global Research Collaborative Work

Traditional drug development often occurs within single companies, limiting resource integration and professional complementarity. Molecule supports modular R&D processes, allowing research teams worldwide to participate in the same project.

IP-NFT holders can flexibly grant access permissions to different research teams, with research data flowing into shared repositories in real-time, promoting cross-team collaboration and knowledge sharing.

#### Commercialization Path: Flexible Value Realization Mechanisms

Molecule's system provides multiple commercialization options that are far more flexible than traditional pathways:

- **Direct Sales**: IP-NFTs can be directly sold to biotech or pharmaceutical companies
- **Spin-off Companies**: New companies can be created based on research results, with IP-NFT holders becoming shareholders
- **Licensing Models**: Technology can be licensed to multiple companies for use, earning licensing fees
- **Hybrid Models**: Partnerships can be established with pharmaceutical companies to share costs and returns

This flexibility allows IP to choose the optimal market path based on specific circumstances, rather than being forced to follow a single model. For researchers, this means more opportunities to realize the value of their research; for investors, it provides diversified return mechanisms.

Molecule's core innovation lies in attaching data and intellectual property rights to non-fungible tokens (NFTs), bringing core IP into the Web3 world, making it liquid, and facilitating its price discovery—both of which are extremely difficult to achieve in today's biotechnology sector.


## IP Tokenization? What is IP-NFTs?

### Deep Understanding of IP-NFTs: More Than Just Digital Certificates

Molecule's core innovation is IP-NFT (Intellectual Property Non-Fungible Tokens). At first glance, this might seem like merely digitalizing ownership certificates for research results, but it actually redefines the ownership structure and value distribution mechanism of scientific research.

Traditionally, transferring research intellectual property is an extremely complex legal process. Contract negotiations can take months or even years, involving multiple lawyers and lengthy documents. IP-NFTs simplify the entire process into transparent blockchain transactions while maintaining legal binding force.

IP-NFTs not only represent ownership but also bundle a series of rights and obligations. When scientists create IP-NFTs on the Molecule platform, they need to clearly define the scope of rights included—complete patent rights, partial usage rights, or data access rights? These terms are encoded into smart contracts, ensuring automatic execution and avoiding future disputes.

Most revolutionary is IP-NFT's fractional ownership model. In traditional models, intellectual property is usually completely owned by a single entity (like a university or company). IP-NFTs allow multiple parties to jointly own research results and share future profits proportionally.

### Technical Depth of IP-NFTs: Smart Contract Architecture

From a technical perspective, Molecule's IP-NFTs are complex combinations of multi-layered smart contracts, far exceeding the complexity of ordinary NFTs. Each IP-NFT contains at least three layers of contract structure:

1. The first layer is the basic NFT contract, ensuring token uniqueness and ownership records. This layer uses the ERC-721 standard but with extensive custom modifications to suit the special needs of scientific IP.
2. The second layer is the data storage contract, responsible for securely storing and managing research-related data. Considering the sensitivity and volume of scientific data, Molecule doesn't directly store data on the blockchain but uses distributed storage solutions (like IPFS and Arweave) combined with encrypted access control.
3. The third layer is the rights management contract, defining ownership distribution, profit sharing, and decision-making weights. This layer implements the most complex functions of IP-NFTs, including fractional ownership, conditional rights transfer, and milestone-triggered fund release.

This multi-layered architecture enables IP-NFTs to handle the complexities of traditional legal agreements while maintaining the transparency and automated execution advantages of blockchain technology. The Molecule platform continuously optimizes these contracts, enhancing their flexibility and security to accommodate the specific needs of different research fields.

### IP Tokens (IPT): Tokenized Shares for Broader Participation

IP Tokens (IPT) are further subdivisions of IP-NFTs that allow broader communities to participate in research projects. If IP-NFTs are like ownership of an entire farm, then IPTs are like small shares of that farm, allowing more people to participate in investment with smaller amounts.

IP-NFT holders can decide to subdivide partial ownership into multiple IPTs. These IPTs can be distributed through public sales, community rewards, and other methods. IPT holders typically have limited rights, such as profit-sharing rights, but may not have decision-making power over research directions.

The main advantages of IPTs are improved liquidity, enabling more people to participate in scientific research investment while providing research teams with additional financing channels.

Currently, there are 9 real tokens that have gone live on the Molecule platform.

## Molecule's Diverse Commercialization Pathways

The Molecule platform supports multiple research commercialization pathways, which is a significant advantage over traditional models. In traditional biotech industries, commercialization paths are typically highly standardized—researchers transfer IP to companies, and companies handle further development and commercialization. Molecule provides more diverse options:

- **Direct Licensing Model** allows IP-NFT holders to license research results to pharmaceutical companies while retaining ownership. For example, in 2022, an IP-NFT for longevity protein research was licensed to a biotech company, but VitaDAO retained the underlying IP ownership and continued to participate in research direction decisions.
- **Spin-off Company Model** allows creating new startups based on IP-NFTs. Unlike traditional models, in this approach, original researchers and early supporters automatically become shareholders of the new company without complex equity negotiations. For example, a neuroscience research team created an IP-NFT through the Molecule platform, later established a new company based on this IP, and original IP-NFT holders received company equity proportionally.
- **Patient-Driven Development Model** is the most innovative pathway. In this model, patient communities can directly invest in research IP-NFTs they care about and participate in development decisions. For example, a rare disease patient group collectively purchased an IP-NFT for a treatment solution through the Molecule platform, then coordinated global patient participation in clinical trials, significantly reducing development costs and time.
- **Hybrid Licensing Model** combines the advantages of open-source and proprietary research. Basic parts of research can be open access to promote broad innovation, while specific applications or optimizations can be protected through IP-NFTs to ensure commercial viability. This model is particularly suitable for basic scientific research, allowing scientists to contribute public knowledge while retaining commercialization pathways.

Currently, this business model is not very profitable yet. The entire company's business model is still in exploration, experimentation, and reshaping phases, and there may be adjustments and changes in the future.


### Real-World Use Cases of Molecule

Molecule has evolved from a theoretical concept to a platform with practical applications, supporting multiple real-world research projects. The following cases demonstrate how the platform operates within the complex scientific research ecosystem:

In August 2021, VitaDAO partnered with the Scheibye-Knudsen lab at the University of Copenhagen to fund a study analyzing 10 molecules with potential life-extending properties. This case marked the first end-to-end IP licensing event between academia and a Web3 organization.

VitaDAO paid for the research expenses and received 70% IP ownership, the university retained 20%, and the research team received 10%. This structure ensured alignment of interests: the research team received funding and retained partial ownership; the university alleviated funding pressure while retaining potential commercial benefits; and the VitaDAO community invested in its core area of interest, gaining primary decision-making power and revenue sharing.

This case is significant not only because it proved the feasibility of the Molecule model but also because it demonstrated how traditional academic institutions can seamlessly collaborate with Web3 organizations. The technology transfer office at the University of Copenhagen had to adapt to a completely new IP handling model, setting an important precedent for future collaborations.

In May 2023, another important case emerged: VitaDAO funded Artan Bio's RNA mutation research project, which was tokenized into an IP-NFT in January 2024 and subsequently evolved into VITARNA IPT. This showcased the complete development cycle, from research proposal and funding approval to IP tokenization and public sale of the IPT. The entire process was transparently recorded on the blockchain, with all decisions and fund flows being traceable.

These cases demonstrate that Molecule is not just a theoretical innovation but a practically viable new model for organizing scientific research. It successfully connects traditional academia with Web3 technology, creating a fairer, more transparent, and more efficient mechanism for scientific funding and IP management.

## BIO Protocol: Financial Infrastructure for Decentralized Science

### The Evolution Relationship Between BIO Protocol and Molecule

Understanding BIO Protocol requires first clarifying its relationship with Molecule. Molecule was initially a unified platform focused on tokenizing scientific IP. As the ecosystem developed, the team recognized the need for specialized infrastructure to support the formation and operation of decentralized science organizations, so BIO Protocol separated from Molecule in 2023 to become an independent protocol.

Molecule focuses on the micro level, providing IP tokenization tools for individual research projects. It solves the problem of "how to convert specific research into digital assets," handling specific operational issues like intellectual property management, data storage, and research commercialization.

BIO Protocol focuses on the macro level, providing organizational and governance frameworks for research communities. It solves the problem of "how to organize people to collectively advance specific research fields," handling systemic issues like community formation, governance rules, fund distribution, and cross-organizational collaboration.

The workflow between the two typically goes like this:

Researchers first join or create a relevant BioDAO through BIO Protocol, forming a community of like-minded individuals. When the community approves a specific research project, the project undergoes IP tokenization through the Molecule platform, creating an IP-NFT. The BioDAO votes to fund the project, acquiring partial ownership of the IP-NFT. During the research process, Molecule provides technical support, ensuring secure storage of research data and protection of IP rights; BIO Protocol offers community support, ensuring continuous funding and cross-disciplinary collaboration.

This division of labor enables the entire system to operate efficiently. Researchers can focus on scientific work without simultaneously worrying about community building and technical details; investors can participate in long-term strategic decisions through the BioDAO while flexibly managing a portfolio of specific project investments; and patients can influence the overall research direction while directly supporting specific treatment projects.

BIO Protocol can be seen as the "financial operating system" of the DeSci space, providing all the tools needed to create, fund, and manage scientific communities. If Molecule is the platform for individual researchers to tokenize their research, then BIO Protocol is the platform that helps organize entire research communities.

### BIO Protocol's Technical Architecture: More Than Just DAO Tools

BIO Protocol's technical architecture far exceeds ordinary DAO toolsets, specifically optimized for the unique needs of scientific research. Its core components include:

- **BioDAO Launchpad** is a platform specifically for creating and launching biotech DAOs. It provides not only technical tools but also legal templates, governance frameworks, and token economics design guides.
- **Liquidity Protocol** is the financial engine of the BIO ecosystem, solving liquidity problems in scientific investment. Traditionally, scientific research investment is viewed as extremely low-liquidity assets, with investors potentially waiting 5-10 years for returns. BIO's liquidity protocol creates secondary markets, allowing early investors to transfer partial holdings before research completion.
- **Cross-DAO Collaboration Framework** allows efficient collaboration between different BioDAOs. Scientific research often requires cross-disciplinary cooperation, and BIO Protocol creates composable collaboration mechanisms allowing multiple DAOs to jointly fund projects, share research data, and distribute profits.

## BioDAO: Scientific Organizations Beyond Traditional DAOs

BioDAOs are not just standard DAOs applied to the scientific field, but entirely new organizational forms designed for the special needs of scientific research. They combine the professionalism of traditional scientific research institutions with the openness and flexibility of Web3 organizations.

BioDAO governance models are typically layered:
1. The first layer is professional review, with scientific committees composed of field experts responsible for evaluating the scientific value and feasibility of research proposals.
2. The second layer is community governance, where token holders vote to decide fund allocation and priorities.

This model balances expertise and community involvement, avoiding two extremes: purely expert decisions that might overlook patient needs, and purely community voting that could lack professional judgment.

Fund management employs multi-signature and phased release mechanisms to ensure fund security and efficient use. Research funding is not disbursed all at once but is released gradually based on pre-set milestones. The release of funds at each stage requires multi-party confirmation, typically including members of the scientific committee, financial managers, and community representatives.

Research data management utilizes blockchain technology to achieve verifiable and traceable research records. Key data and results generated during the research process are stored in an encrypted manner within a distributed system, ensuring data integrity and transparency while protecting sensitive information and intellectual property.

Long-term sustainability is ensured through various mechanisms. In addition to the initial funding obtained through token sales, BioDAOs also establish continuous revenue streams through IP licensing fees, commercialization revenue sharing, and service fees. Some BioDAOs have even established perpetual endowment fund structures, similar to university endowments, to ensure a long-term stable source of funding.

### Diversity of the BioDAO Ecosystem: Focusing on Different Research Fields

BIO Protocol has already incubated multiple BioDAOs focused on different fields, demonstrating the broad applicability and innovative potential of the model:

VitaDAO, one of the earliest BioDAOs established, focuses on longevity research. It has become a benchmark case in the DeSci space, with over 100 active scientists and more than 2000 community members. VitaDAO's innovation lies in its redefinition of priorities in longevity research, directing funding towards areas often overlooked by traditional funding institutions, such as epigenetics and metabolic regulation. As of 2024, VitaDAO has funded over 15 research projects, totaling more than $2 million.

AthenaDAO focuses on women's health research, addressing gender bias in traditional research. For a long time, research on diseases specific to women has been underfunded, and AthenaDAO is changing this. Its governance structure ensures that women have a dominant voice in the decision-making process, which is extremely rare in traditional research institutions. AthenaDAO has funded multiple research projects on menstrual health, menopause, and women's diseases, creating a new paradigm for women's health research.

PsyDAO explores the application of psychedelic drugs in treating mental health issues. This field has long been neglected by mainstream research due to regulatory and social biases, and PsyDAO is breaking this barrier. Its strategy is to support early preclinical research, laying the groundwork for subsequent clinical trials. PsyDAO's innovations also include a patient data ownership model, where patients participating in research can control the use of their personal data and share in the value of research outcomes.

This diverse ecosystem of BioDAOs demonstrates how decentralized science models can adapt to the needs of different research fields and address important issues neglected in traditional scientific research models. Each BioDAO can design unique governance and funding models tailored to the characteristics of its own field, maintaining the core principles of decentralized science while adapting to the specific needs of different scientific domains.

### The Potential Impact of BIO Protocol: Reshaping the Landscape of Scientific Research

BIO Protocol's goal extends far beyond creating a few decentralized organizations; it aims to reshape the entire organization and funding model of scientific research. If successfully scaled, it could have a profound impact on the global research ecosystem:

The democratization of research priorities is perhaps the most revolutionary change. Traditionally, research directions are primarily determined by government agencies, large foundations, and pharmaceutical companies, often overlooking public needs. The BioDAO model allows a broader range of stakeholders, including patients, to directly participate in decision-making, potentially transforming research priorities. For example, research on rare diseases may receive more attention as patients can collectively mobilize resources, no longer solely relying on commercial interests.

Improved efficiency in funding allocation is another key impact. The traditional scientific research funding process is bureaucratic and slow, often taking 12-18 months to reach a decision. The BioDAO model has already demonstrated the ability to shorten this timeframe to a few weeks, significantly accelerating research progress. At the same time, the phased funding release mechanism ensures that resources are directed towards projects that truly yield results, improving overall efficiency.

The formation of global collaboration networks is another important impact. BioDAOs break down geographical and institutional barriers, allowing researchers worldwide to collaborate seamlessly. For example, a computational biology lab in India could partner with clinical researchers in Germany and patient organizations in the United States to jointly develop new therapies, something extremely difficult to achieve in the traditional system.

In the long term, BIO Protocol could foster entirely new scientific economic models, changing the way scientific knowledge is produced, shared, and commercialized. If successful, it will become another major transformation in the history of science, following the open access movement, driving science towards a more open, equitable, and efficient future.

## Molecule and BIO: Complementary DeSci Infrastructure

To fully understand the relationship between Molecule and BIO Protocol, we need to delve deeper into how they work together to support the decentralized science ecosystem.

### Complementary Division of Labor

Molecule and BIO Protocol form a complete decentralized science infrastructure:

Molecule's expertise lies in the tokenization and marketization of research IP. It focuses on developing the technical framework that enables researchers to:
- Transform research outcomes into tradable IP-NFTs
- Ensure the integrity and verifiability of IP
- Establish the infrastructure for IP trading and licensing
- Provide mechanisms for IP valuation and discovery
- BIO Protocol's expertise, on the other hand, lies in community organization and funding allocation. It focuses on:
  - Incubating and supporting the establishment of various BioDAOs
  - Providing governance frameworks for BioDAOs
  - Creating token economic models for BioDAOs

## The Future Vision: Reshaping the Global Scientific Research Landscape

Looking at the development paths of Molecule and BIO Protocol and the challenges they face, we can outline the future vision of the DeSci ecosystem and its potential profound impact on the global scientific research landscape.

In the short term (1-3 years), we may see DeSci move from niche experimentation to initial mainstreaming. More traditional academic institutions will experiment with DeSci models, establishing cooperative relationships with BioDAOs.

In the medium term (3-5 years), DeSci may achieve deep integration with traditional scientific research systems. We may see hybrid funding models become the norm, with research projects simultaneously receiving traditional grants and DAO funding.

In the long term (5-10 years), DeSci may completely reshape the global scientific research landscape. We may witness entirely new forms of scientific organization emerging, combining the professionalism of traditional academic institutions with the open collaborative characteristics of Web3 organizations.

The democratization of research priorities may be the most revolutionary change. Traditionally, research directions were mainly determined by government agencies, large foundations, and pharmaceutical companies, often ignoring public needs. The BioDAO model allows broader stakeholders (including patients) to directly participate in decision-making, potentially completely changing research priorities.

Ultimately, the DeSci vision is to create a more open, fair, and efficient scientific ecosystem, making science truly become "science of the people, for the people." Molecule and BIO Protocol, as core infrastructure of this ecosystem, are turning this vision into reality, step by step changing how we organize, fund, and conduct scientific research.
