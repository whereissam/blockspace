# Challenges and Future of Web3 Social Products

Blockchain technology and cryptocurrencies have created innovative possibilities for traditional internet applications. Web3 social products, as a key component, carry the vision of redefining digital social interaction through decentralization. However, many Web3 social platforms have struggled to maintain momentum, often appearing as fleeting phenomena. This article explores the core challenges facing Web3 social products and their potential future directions.

## The Evolution of Web3 Social Products

Web3 social products have undergone multiple cycles of exploration in the cryptocurrency realm. From the earliest Bitcoin community protocol Nostr supported by Twitter founder Jack, to the Lens protocol launched by the DeFi protocol Aave team, to Farcaster heavily invested by A16Z, various platforms have attempted to establish new decentralized social models. These platforms clearly demonstrate the changing directions and priorities in the Web3 social space.

![](https://techcrunch.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/image1.png)

Farcaster, as the current mainstream Web3 social protocol, has experienced brand changes from Farcaster to Warpcast and back to Farcaster, with an evolution path that is highly representative. Initially focused on decentralized technology attributes and data sovereignty, it later had to gradually enhance asset properties by launching features like Frames and built-in wallets. ZORA has launched Content Coin, completely tokenizing content by issuing an ERC-20 token corresponding to each piece of content, making it directly tradable in trading pools.

## The Dilemma of Value Discovery and Pricing Mechanisms

The most fundamental challenge for Web3 social products lies in establishing reasonable value discovery and pricing mechanisms for creator content. Traditional social media primarily relies on advertising revenue models, with creators monetizing through traffic, creating tension between quality content and commercialization. Creators often need to publish multiple pieces of high-quality content while interspersing marketing content with soft advertising to generate income, creating potential conflicts between creation and profit objectives.

Web3 social attempts to create new economic models through tokenization. Friend.tech, which gained popularity in 2023, implemented a Bonding Curve mechanism allowing users to "purchase" each other's social tokens, forming a mutually beneficial economic system. Meanwhile, the Degen feature in the Farcaster ecosystem achieved significant user growth through a token tipping mechanism. However, the sustainability of these models faces challenges, primarily because their fundamental driving force stems from speculative mentality rather than the intrinsic value of the content.

The Base protocol supported by Coinbase advocates that all digital content should be tokenized, a concept that has sparked extensive discussion in the industry. This comprehensive tokenization approach may lead platforms to deviate from their core social functions. Content token models on platforms like ZORA have been analyzed as emphasizing transactions over content consumption, shifting users' focus from appreciating content to monitoring token price fluctuations.

## Difficulties in User Attraction and Retention

Decentralized social protocols like Farcaster face significant challenges in attracting and retaining ordinary users, with the core issue being their inability to provide sufficient differentiated value. Traditional platforms like Twitter or Weibo have large user bases and mature recommendation algorithms, making it easier for new users to gain attention and build influence. In contrast, new users on platforms like Farcaster often struggle to feel a sense of "belonging" and find it difficult to truly integrate into the community.

More critically, emerging platforms as small teams cannot compete with large platforms in recommendation algorithms, resulting in content primarily serving the earliest batch of users—mainly investment circles and early community members. This closed small-circle characteristic makes it difficult for platforms to break through growth bottlenecks, creating a predicament where they cannot cater to different groups.

Moreover, while the concept of decentralization is important, it does not provide sufficient attraction for most users. Ordinary users find it difficult to pay for "data sovereignty" because they are accustomed to using traditional platforms for free. This leads Web3 social platforms to often turn to token incentives and airdrops to acquire users when growth stalls, as evidenced by the trend from initially opposing excessive financialization to later relying on airdrops to drive daily active users.

## Limitations of Technical Infrastructure and User Experience

Current blockchain infrastructure still has notable deficiencies in supporting the scale requirements of social media. Social platforms are characterized by high-frequency interactions and massive data, requiring high performance. Twitter generates approximately 500-600 million pieces of data daily, and if all this data were to be converted into smart contracts on the blockchain, current infrastructure falls far short of the requirements in terms of performance, cost, and convenience.

Specifically, Web3 social protocols like Farcaster essentially provide two services: the ability for users to broadcast content, and the storage of user identity, social graph, and content data in a multi-node network. However, for users, the former is already free on traditional platforms; the latter, while providing data sovereignty, has limited perceived value and requires payment. In the trade-off between "free service VS paid sovereignty," most users naturally choose the former.

Furthermore, Web3 social products also have significant gaps in user experience. Many users describe Farcaster as "looking like a less user-friendly Twitter." This experience gap, combined with the lack of network effects, makes it difficult for Web3 social products to compete with mature platforms.

## Fundamental Contradictions Between Product Positioning and Economic Models

The deeper issue facing Web3 social products is the fundamental contradiction between product positioning and economic models. Observing their development trajectories, almost all platforms have undergone a transformation from "rejecting excessive financialization" to "gradually strengthening financial attributes."

This phenomenon highlights the core dilemma of Web3 social: without emphasizing tokens and wealth effects, it's difficult to attract a user base; overly emphasizing financial attributes may deviate from the essence of social platforms. Practice shows that at the current stage, the main driver of user participation often does not stem from concepts of decentralization or data sovereignty, but from wealth effects associated with assets, manifesting as the prioritization of the "Fi" (Finance) element in the "Social Fi" concept.

The definition and discovery mechanism of content value constitutes a more fundamental problem. A key question worth considering is: what is the source of social content's value? Is it the quality of the content itself? Is it the reach and attention gained? Or is it the identity and social influence of the publisher? In an era where artificial intelligence can produce large amounts of content and digital information is easily replicated, defining the value of content itself is increasingly ambiguous, making the design of value discovery mechanisms for Web3 social more complex.

## Innovation Exploration and Future Directions

Despite the numerous challenges facing Web3 social products, various innovative attempts continue. ZORA's content tokens, though controversial, represent a radical attempt to completely tokenize data; various social protocols continue to optimize decentralized architectures and explore ways to lower user thresholds; while some teams focus on building more suitable product experiences for specific language communities.

The podcast discussion mentioned the concept of "Coin as Service," suggesting that future blockchain networks might support the issuance and application of hundreds of thousands or even millions of tokens daily. When every piece of social content, every social circle, and every user identity can be tokenized efficiently and at low cost, true mass adoption might be achieved.

In the Chinese-speaking region, Web3 social exploration also holds unique value. Chinese teams may have advantages in the social application field, unlike the obvious disadvantages they face in underlying protocol development. However, cultural differences in innovation also bring challenges—Western entrepreneurs may tend to approach with an open experimental mindset for diverse exploration, while Chinese teams might excel at achieving extreme optimization and refinement after a model has been validated.

## Conclusion: Finding Breakthroughs Through Countless Attempts

The development of Web3 social products remains in an exploratory stage, with industry discussions reflecting that a fully validated successful approach has yet to be found. However, this is precisely the process of innovation—through countless attempts and failures, breakthrough solutions may eventually emerge.

The key to future success lies in how to better integrate the value of social behaviors and content dissemination with token mechanisms, creating product experiences that truly attract users. This requires more experimentation and attempts, as emphasized by the radical perspective in the podcast, different approaches, even counter-consensus ones, are worth trying.

The future path of Web3 social products will inevitably be full of challenges, but also contains infinite possibilities. Perhaps one day, an innovative model that truly balances social experience with token economics, content value with user incentives, will be discovered, fundamentally changing our digital social methods. Until then, various innovative attempts, whether successful or not, are all necessary paths leading to this future.
