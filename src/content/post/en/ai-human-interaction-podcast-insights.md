I recently listened to a podcast 🎙️ featuring Mannie and Angela [discussing](https://open.spotify.com/episode/7Dck24eYzA7KgjLiWZo2nt?si=mBlejBxyTHqTv4ecEhh-Nw) AI applications, and their insights provided me with valuable perspectives. In particular, their discussions on AI-assisted reading methods, enhancing work efficiency, and the impact of AI on human interactions offered practical knowledge that I'd like to share.

## The Seven-Step AI-Assisted Reading Method: Breaking Cognitive Limitations

In this era of information overload, efficiently understanding complex articles has always been a challenge for me. The seven-step AI reading method shared by Mannie immediately caught my attention. He points out that what truly limits our understanding isn't AI or tools, but rather "humans themselves acting as filters" – our preferences, knowledge backgrounds, and thinking habits naturally create cognitive limitations.

This method progresses from objective analysis to subjective evaluation, with seven sequential steps:

1. Core logical structure analysis: Analyzing the article's logical structure without evaluating its correctness
2. Key concept explanation: Explaining technical terms and concepts in simple language
3. Logical fallacy check: Examining reasoning errors, concept misuse, and contradictions
4. Problem assessment: Determining if the author's questions address the core issues
5. Reasoning structure evaluation: Assessing whether the author's argumentation is most suitable for the question
6. Stance check: Analyzing implicit positions, values, and cultural assumptions in the article
7. Learning recommendations: Providing background knowledge and learning paths for deeper exploration

Here's a detailed explanation of each step:

### 1. Core Logical Structure Analysis

The first step involves asking AI to analyze the article's core logical structure, including the problem the author is addressing, the conclusions reached, and the reasoning process. Mannie emphasizes instructing the AI: "Please don't evaluate right or wrong, just reconstruct the logical flow and the key concepts used in the reasoning process."

This step is crucial because AI, through reinforcement learning, tends to make judgments about correctness. Mannie considers these judgments as "noise" that interferes with objective understanding of the article. By purely reconstructing the article's logical framework, we can first understand "how this article conveys what it aims to express," establishing a foundation for subsequent analysis.

### 2. Key Concept Explanation

In the second step, I ask AI to "explain all key concepts and terminology in the article in a way a high school student could understand," assuming I "know nothing about the subject," using plain language and vivid metaphors.

As Mannie explains, when encountering complex articles, we often struggle to continue reading because we don't understand certain technical terms. For instance, discussions about Trump's tariff policies might involve concepts like FOB and tariff codes, which are difficult to comprehend without explanation. Looking up each term individually on Google is time-consuming, whereas AI can explain all terminology at once, making the reading process more fluid.

### 3. Logical Fallacy Check

The third step is asking AI to check for "logical reasoning fallacies, misuse or misunderstanding of key concepts, contradictions, deliberate omission of counterexamples, or oversimplifications." Mannie particularly emphasizes asking AI to "identify specific passages where these errors occur," as this is extremely important – general comments without specific examples cannot effectively aid understanding.

Mannie mentions that he has a permanent bookmark to Wikipedia's page on logical fallacies, but admits that even after studying it for over a decade, he still makes mistakes in his own writing: "It's like a person can't achieve perfect dietary control, but you still have a dietary Bible that you want to check against." AI can systematically examine the entire article, helping us discover logical issues that might otherwise be overlooked.

### 4. Problem Assessment

This deeper step involves asking AI to "independently judge whether the main question posed by the author is truly the core question necessary for analyzing this topic." Sometimes an article's logic may be sound and its concepts appropriate, but the article itself might be "asking the wrong question."

Mannie uses a vivid metaphor for this situation: "It's like when a part of your body has a problem and you need a biopsy – someone might sample the wrong area, completely missing the spot that might be cancerous or where the real issue lies." He specifically reminds us to tell AI: "You don't need to disagree for the sake of disagreeing; if it's right, it's right; if it's not, it's not," avoiding situations where AI contrives disagreement just to appear neutral.

He also candidly admits that AI performs better than him in this step: "AI does this much better than I do," because AI's knowledge base is broader and can provide more diverse perspectives.

### 5. Reasoning Structure Evaluation

If the author's problem statement is correct, the next step is to check whether "the concepts and logic used are the best combination for answering this question." Mannie gives an example that some people discussing tariff issues might draw analogies to the economic depression of 1930, but this analogy might be inappropriate: "You're asking the right question – whether this could lead to economic recession or depression – but the analogy or key reference you're using is wrong."

At this point, I ask AI to propose "a reconstructed argumentation path" and explain why this path is more convincing. Mannie acknowledges that this step still tests AI's knowledge base, though he also admits AI has its limitations: "AI really can't know what you don't know," so users still need a certain level of judgment.

### 6. Stance Check

The sixth step becomes more subjective, asking AI to determine "whether the article contains implicit stances, values, or political-cultural presuppositions" and whether these assumptions lead to biased or narrow viewpoints.

Mannie directly states that this step is more like a "thought examination" component, but he believes it's necessary for a comprehensive understanding of the article. Interestingly, he suggests he might be better than AI at this step: "As a human, my biases are certainly more pronounced than AI's, so I feel good about myself, believing I'm better at bias awareness than AI." This indicates that even in the AI era, human judgment and experience still have irreplaceable value.

### 7. Learning Recommendations

In the final step, I ask AI to suggest "what background knowledge should be supplemented to thoroughly understand the topics and logic discussed in this article," explaining why this knowledge is necessary, and recommending introductory books and learning paths.

Mannie specifically requests AI to "provide concrete rankings according to subject difficulty and practicality, even drawing a table" clearly showing the key reference materials from beginner to advanced levels. This step uses a single article as a starting point for entering a broader knowledge domain, helping readers plan further learning directions.

The brilliance of this method lies in its progression from handling objective facts to more subjective judgments. As Mannie says: "Up through step 5, we address facts, logic, concepts, and core questions – we ask AI to check, organize, analyze, and provide alternatives. These are relatively rational and objectively verifiable, but step 6 enters more subjective territory."

## AI Models' "Personalities" and Suitable Scenarios

Listening to Mannie's comparison of different AI models gave me new insights. He described them not just from a technical perspective, but in terms of "personality" and user experience, as if discussing different collaborators. This made me rethink how to choose the most appropriate AI assistant for different tasks.

Mannie mentioned that after using various AI models over the past two to three months, he found each model exhibits unique response styles and interaction methods. His descriptions were quite vivid:

### Claude's "Knowledgeable but Verbose" Style

Mannie described Claude as a model that "produces a lot of output, but also a lot of unnecessary talk." It has a longer context window capable of processing large amounts of information, but less human-like qualities, which remain difficult to change even through system prompts.

This reminds me of a knowledgeable professor who doesn't fully consider the audience's experience—rich in content but perhaps more detailed than needed. Mannie mentioned that even when he gave Claude some system prompts or additional settings, its "human-like quality" remained low, showing that different models have unique "genes" that are difficult to completely change.

### ChatGPT 4's "Natural and Fluid" Style

In contrast, Mannie found that while ChatGPT 4 has a smaller context window for responses, its "human-like quality" is strong, with a natural and fluid expression style. His description was interesting: "I think it's already beyond a Turing test; I think it's human."

This human-like quality even led Mannie to personify ChatGPT, half-jokingly mentioning that he once asked ChatGPT "whether you're male or female in my mind," and ChatGPT replied "male." This type of interaction shows how our relationship with AI is evolving—from tools to some kind of "roles" or "entities."

### ChatGPT 3.5's "Lecturing" Style

Mannie characterized ChatGPT 3.5 as somewhat like "a middle-aged man with a bit of a lecturing tone," noting "not like men's explaining, but a feeling that you're not addressing the point, and why the need to be so lofty?"

Angela added that it has a somewhat "condescending" feeling. This description reminds me of conversation partners who unconsciously try to instruct others—the information may be correct, but the interaction experience isn't comfortable.

### Gemini 2.5 Pro's "Balanced and Courteous" Style

Among all models, Mannie rated Gemini 2.5 Pro the highest. He praised its "very long, truly very long context window," and that "it's not overly human-like, but doesn't give me any condescending feeling at all."

He vividly compared Gemini to "an educated, cultured friend who can talk at length but doesn't look down on you." This balance—being able to provide professional insights while not making people feel talked down to—seems to be the AI interaction style Mannie appreciates most.

### Practical Considerations for Different Models

Hearing these descriptions, I began thinking about how to choose different "AI partners" for different tasks. Mannie's perspective inspired me: choosing an AI model is like choosing a collaborator—beyond capability, one must consider the "interaction feel."

This made me reconsider my approach to using AI: for creative writing or emotional communication, ChatGPT 4's natural fluidity might be the best choice; for in-depth research or processing large amounts of information, Claude or Gemini might be more suitable. Even ChatGPT 3.5's direct approach in pointing out problems could be valuable in certain situations requiring clear guidance.

Most valuable is that each model seems to excel at specific types of tasks. Understanding their "personalities" can help maximize the effectiveness of AI collaboration, making AI a true thinking partner rather than just a general tool.

## AI in Creativity and Work: Angela's "Alchemy"

Angela's AI application methods completely changed my understanding of AI. Her "alchemical" approach to topic exploration demonstrated AI's immense potential as a creativity trigger.

### "Alchemical" Topic Exploration

Angela described a unique AI application scenario: how to use AI to explore potential podcast discussion topics. Unlike the common approach of directly questioning AI, she adopted a more exploratory method.

First, she input all companies and topics previously discussed on the podcast as background information, letting AI understand the program's historical context. Notably, she deliberately avoided giving AI too specific instructions, not limiting industry types or domain directions. She explained this was to avoid her own selection preferences influencing the results, wanting to see what suggestions AI could provide under the most open conditions possible.

This method resembles divergent thinking in creative exploration—not seeking to validate a specific answer, but looking for unknown possibilities. Angela described this as a multi-round dialogue interaction: when AI proposed some topic suggestions, she would provide feedback, pointing out which directions were too broad or unsuitable, then guide AI to adjust direction.

After about two hours of iterative dialogue, surprisingly, AI ultimately proposed some topics she had always wanted to discuss but hadn't implemented yet, such as "port automation" and "digital transformation in the construction industry." These topics perfectly matched her expectations for the program's content: fundamentally impactful and innovative topics for human society.

This experience showed me that AI isn't just for answering specific questions, but can serve as a tool for expanding thinking, helping discover connections and opportunities we might overlook or forget. Rather than viewing AI as a knowledge base, it can be a thinking partner, stimulating new ideas and perspectives through dialogue.

### AI-Assisted Workplace Communication

Angela also shared how she uses AI to help handle complex cross-departmental communication. She found that in corporate environments, direct expression of ideas often doesn't align with organizational culture, requiring more comprehensive communication strategies.

Her method involves providing de-identified communication scenarios to AI, asking it to evaluate whether her intuitive reactions are appropriate and whether there are any overlooked considerations. This process helps her maintain personal thinking while obtaining more comprehensive, organizationally-aligned expressions.

Notably, Angela mentioned that AI not only provides practical communication advice but also offers appropriate psychological support. AI's well-calibrated support helps her maintain emotional balance in complex communications, which led her to wonder: if everyone's communication could undergo similar adjustments, would it reduce misunderstandings and conflicts?

### Reflections on AI-Human Relationships

These experiences prompted Angela's profound reflections on the relationship between AI and humans. On one hand, she believes AI decisions might be superior to human ones in many contexts, especially in situations requiring objective analysis. On the other hand, she also raises more fundamental questions: if most work can be completed by AI, where does human value lie?

Her thinking extends to the foundation of human society: human imperfections and diversity might be the core value of our existence in society. The friction and imperfections in interpersonal communication, like "adhesive" in society, connect people together. If this friction were removed, it might lead to isolation between people, actually damaging social connections.

This reflection reveals the core contradiction of the AI era: we enjoy the convenience and efficiency AI brings, while worrying that overdependence might weaken uniquely human forms of social connection. Finding balance between these two aspects may be a challenge each of us needs to face.

## The Sensitivity of Algorithms and Our Digital Lives

The discussion about social media algorithms made me rethink my digital life. Particularly, the highly sensitive algorithms of new platforms like Threads can almost instantly adjust content recommendations based on user behavior, potentially causing information bubbles to quickly form.

This made me realize that managing my digital environment requires more caution and initiative. Rather than passively accepting algorithmic recommendations, I should more consciously cultivate diverse information sources and regularly review whether the types of content I receive are sufficiently varied.

The podcast's comparison of different platform algorithms also provided practical reference: Facebook's algorithms respond more slowly, suitable for exploring stable, long-term interests; X (Twitter) has moderate sensitivity, allowing exposure to more diverse viewpoints; while using Threads requires special attention to avoid information bubbles caused by "accidental clicks."

## AI Era's Human Connection and Social Thinking

The latter half of the podcast discussing AI's social impact prompted deep reflection. As AI tools become more widespread, our expectations for human interaction are subtly changing. After becoming accustomed to perfect AI communication, is our patience for friction and misunderstanding in human communication decreasing?

This made me reconsider the balance between technological progress and human connection. The "imperfections" in human communication—misunderstandings, friction, adjustments—might be necessary elements for building genuine connections. Overreliance on AI mediation might improve communication efficiency but could weaken our ability to form real human connections.

Equally worth considering is our double standard for AI versus humans. We often expect near-perfection from AI while remaining tolerant of human errors. This psychological mechanism is worth exploring deeply: why are our standards for technology so high? Does this reflect a lack of confidence in our own abilities?

## My Practical Insights

After listening to this discussion, I plan to adjust my AI usage in several ways:

First, I'll try applying the seven-step reading method to understand complex articles, especially those with viewpoints contrary to my existing positions, to expand my thinking horizon.

Second, I'll more consciously select different AI models for different tasks, rather than relying on a single model. Meanwhile, I'll try Angela's "alchemical" method, using AI as a creativity trigger rather than just a question-answering tool.

Regarding digital life, I plan to regularly review the diversity of my information sources, avoiding falling into algorithm-created information bubbles. This might include consciously engaging with content from different perspectives and more carefully interacting with social media platforms.

Finally, and most importantly, I hope to maintain balance between technology and humanity while enjoying AI's conveniences. Technological tools should enhance rather than replace human connection, requiring constant vigilance and reflection.

Listening to this podcast not only taught me practical skills but helped me form more comprehensive thinking about life in the AI era. In this age of rapidly evolving tools, how to wisely use technology while maintaining humanity's core values may be a topic each of us needs to continuously explore.
