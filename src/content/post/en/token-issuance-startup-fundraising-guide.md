# Beyond Venture Capital: Start Your Fundraising Journey with Token Issuance

In today's competitive startup ecosystem, fundraising remains one of the biggest challenges entrepreneurs face. Traditionally, venture capital (VC) has been the primary source of funding for tech startups, but with the development of blockchain technology, token issuance financing has become an extremely attractive alternative. This article will analyze in depth how to raise funds through token sales and explore various aspects of this option.

![image](https://i.imgur.com/n9jtFtQ.png)

## Limitations of Traditional Venture Capital

Venture capital, as a mature financing method, has supported countless successful tech companies over the years. However, it also has obvious drawbacks. First, the fundraising process is typically extremely lengthy. Most entrepreneurs need to spend months on pitching, negotiation, and due diligence—time that could otherwise be used for business development and product improvement.

Second, venture capitalists usually demand considerable control. In a typical financing round, entrepreneurs may need to give up about 20% of company ownership. More importantly, venture capitalists often require special rights, such as liquidation preferences, board seats, and veto rights on major decisions. These terms can severely limit founders' autonomy.

Finally, venture capital funds have extremely high expectations for returns. A typical VC fund needs to return about 3x its investment amount, but since many investments will fail, successful portfolio companies actually need to provide returns of up to 100x. This pressure may force entrepreneurs to make short-sighted decisions, sacrificing long-term value.

## Unique Advantages of Token Fundraising

In contrast, token fundraising offers several significant advantages. First, it allows access to a broader investor base. Traditional venture capital comes mainly from professional investment institutions, while token sales can attract retail and institutional investors globally, creating a more diverse source of funding.

Liquidity is another important advantage. In traditional venture capital, investors typically need to wait several years to realize an exit through acquisition or IPO. In the token model, investors can immediately buy and sell on exchanges after the token is listed, greatly increasing the attractiveness of the investment.

The token model also creates better incentive alignment. Investors are not only providers of funds but may also become early users and promoters of the product. Their success is directly related to the product's adoption rate and usage, creating a mutually beneficial relationship.

Additionally, founders typically retain more control over their company. Without complicated VC terms, founders can more freely develop the company according to their own vision while still obtaining the needed funding.

## Best Timing for Token Fundraising

Token fundraising is not suitable for all stages of a company. In the earliest stage, when a company has only an idea or prototype, token sales are usually not the best choice. Investors want to see a certain degree of product realization and market validation, unless the founder has a very successful entrepreneurial track record.

Token fundraising is most suitable for seed rounds and A-B-C rounds. At these stages, the company has already proven its product concept, has an initial user base, but needs funding to scale and accelerate growth. This is also the stage where traditional venture capital would be considered, so token fundraising provides a direct alternative.

According to Cryptorank data, approximately $2.561 billion has been raised through token sales in 2024, distributed across 824 successful ICO and IDO projects, with a median fundraising amount of $500,000 per project. This data demonstrates a significant resurgence of token fundraising as a financing method, although still below the historical peak of 2017 ($9.063 billion), but represents a stable recovery since 2021.

Looking at historical trends, the token fundraising market has experienced noticeable cyclical changes: 2017-2018 was the first boom period, followed by a downturn in 2019-2020, a second boom in 2021 (raising $4.387 billion across a record 962 projects), followed by another adjustment period in 2022-2023. The current 2024 market environment shows signs of healthy growth, with more reasonable average project sizes, focusing more on substance rather than speculation.

It's worth noting that while token sales account for only about 2% of traditional venture capital totals, if only early-stage financing is considered, the proportion could be as high as 10%, indicating this has become a fundraising channel that cannot be ignored, especially for blockchain and Web3-related projects.

## In-depth Analysis of Token Types

![image](https://i.imgur.com/NmWHVgf.png)

The current tokenized asset market has a total value exceeding $500 billion, distributed across six major categories. Understanding these different types of tokens and their market sizes is crucial for founders to choose the appropriate token type:

1. Stablecoins: Market cap of $205 billion, the largest token category. These tokens are typically pegged to fiat currencies (such as the USD), providing price stability in the cryptocurrency world. Major examples include USDT (Tether), USDC, and BNB.
2. Utility and DAO tokens: Market cap of $136 billion, the second largest category. These tokens give holders rights to use specific services or voting rights in decentralized organizations.
3. Memecoins: Market cap of $127 billion, although initially viewed as jokes, they have become an important part of the market. These include Dogecoin and Shiba Inu.
4. Non-fungible tokens (NFTs): Market cap of $31 billion, representing unique digital assets, from artwork to in-game items. Major platforms include Opensea and Rarible.
5. Real world assets tokens: Market cap of $18 billion, representing real-world assets such as real estate or commodities.
6. Security tokens: Market cap of $13 billion, representing tokenized versions of traditional financial securities, such as equity or debt. Mainly issued by institutions like Standard Chartered and the European Investment Bank.

This diversified token ecosystem provides founders with various fundraising options, but for most startups, utility tokens and security tokens are the most relevant categories. Below, we'll explore these two main types in more depth.

### Utility Tokens

Utility tokens are currently the most common type of token, giving holders the right to use certain products or services. Taking Binance's BNB token as an example, holders can use BNB to pay transaction fees, get discounts, participate in token issuances, etc. The value of utility tokens is typically directly related to the usage and success of the underlying platform.

Utility tokens don't promise any fixed returns or dividends, but investors expect that as platform usage increases, token demand will rise, thus driving up prices. This creates a natural growth mechanism that encourages early investors to also become platform promoters.

In terms of regulation, utility tokens have long been in a relatively gray area, but Europe's recent MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets) regulations have begun to regulate utility tokens. Nevertheless, they are still far less regulated than security tokens, making them the preferred choice for most crypto projects.

### Security Tokens

Security tokens are essentially tokenized versions of traditional securities, representing company equity (similar to stocks) or debt (similar to bonds). Unlike utility tokens, security tokens typically promise investors dividends, interest, or other forms of financial returns.

Issuing security tokens requires compliance with strict securities regulations, including preparing detailed prospectuses (typically 60-80 pages long), providing audited financial statements, and adhering to various investor protection measures. This significantly increases issuance costs, typically requiring €30,000 to €80,000 in legal fees.

Large enterprises like Siemens have issued security tokens (such as a €100 million tokenized bond), but for most startups, utility tokens remain a more practical choice unless their business model is particularly suited to a security structure.

## Six Key Steps in Token Fundraising

### 1. Define the Project and Token Purpose

First, you need to clearly define the company's business and the purpose of the token. For utility tokens, you need to think about how it naturally integrates into the product ecosystem. What rights or service access will the token give to holders? How does it promote platform adoption and use?

For security tokens, you need to decide whether to offer equity or debt, and what kind of returns (dividends or interest) to promise. These decisions will shape your entire token structure and sales strategy.

You also need to clearly plan how the raised funds will be used. Investors want to know how their money will be used to drive business growth, whether for product development, marketing, or team expansion.

### 2. Design Token Economics and Sale Type

Token economics determine how tokens are distributed and the release schedule, which is crucial for the long-term health of the token. You need to determine the total supply, distribution ratios, and locking and unlocking schedules.

Taking Uniswap as an example, its UNI token allocated 60% to the community, indicating its emphasis on users. They designed a four-year gradual unlocking plan to ensure tokens wouldn't flood the market in a short period.

The token sale type also needs careful consideration:
- ICO (Initial Coin Offering) is the traditional method, selling utility tokens to the public, but doesn't guarantee secondary market liquidity. Sometimes combined with exchange listings (IEO), but exchange listing fees are usually high.
- IDO (Initial DEX Offering) is the currently recommended method, automatically allocating a portion of the raised funds to decentralized exchange liquidity pools through smart contracts, providing immediate trading capability. This gives investors more confidence because they know they can sell tokens when needed.
- STO (Security Token Offering) is for public sales of security tokens, requiring compliance with strict securities regulations. While the regulatory burden is heavier, it may be a necessary choice for certain business models.
- Fair Launch sells the entire token supply with no reserves for the team or early investors. This approach is less commonly used for fundraising but more common when issuing network tokens or memecoins.

### 3. Prepare Professional Issuance Materials

Issuance materials are the window to showcase the project's value to potential investors and are also important legal protection documents. Depending on the token type, you need to prepare a whitepaper (utility tokens) or securities prospectus (security tokens).

The whitepaper should describe in detail the project, market problem, solution, technical implementation, team background, token economics, and use of funds. Like the Basic Attention Token whitepaper, it primarily focuses on problems in the digital advertising industry and its solution based on the Brave browser, with token issuance being just part of the overall strategy.

A securities prospectus is a more complex legal document that needs to be prepared by professional lawyers, containing detailed risk disclosures, audited financial statements, and strict compliance information.

Legally, unless you have absolute certainty and professional guidance, it's advisable to exclude US investors from token sales, as US securities regulations take an especially strict stance on crypto tokens.

### 4. Build Solid Operational Infrastructure

Token sales require complex technical and operational infrastructure, but the good news is that there are many ready-made solutions available now.

First, you need to choose a blockchain, with Solana, Ethereum, and Base being the currently most active choices. If conducting an IDO, you also need to select a decentralized exchange suitable for your chosen blockchain, such as Raydium on Solana or Uniswap on Ethereum.

When creating tokens and smart contracts, you can use token generators like Bitbond's Token Tool, configuring through a user interface rather than coding. These tools typically provide audited contract templates, greatly reducing security risks.

You also need to set up token locking mechanisms, implement KYC/AML processes, deploy sales smart contracts, prepare investor relations management systems, and build website and marketing infrastructure.

For companies without large communities, consider using launchpads like DAO Maker or VXL App, which not only provide technical infrastructure but also bring potential investors, charging about 5% of the raised funds as fees.

### 5. Execute a Comprehensive Marketing Strategy

Marketing may be the most critical factor for token sale success. First, you need to clearly define the target investor group, then prepare marketing materials including whitepaper, lightweight whitepaper, professional website, roadmap, and team introduction.

The most important marketing channel is nurturing the existing community. Engage with the community through social media, newsletters, and webinars to make them advocates for the token sale. Other channels include bounty programs and airdrops, ICO calendars and listings, PR and media coverage, opinion leader collaborations, paid advertising, and centralized exchange listings (although fees may be as high as €200,000-300,000).

Marketing campaigns should convey the project's credibility, team strength, fairness of token economics, and alignment of incentive mechanisms to build investor confidence.

### 6. Carefully Execute the Sale and Follow-up Activities

Token sales typically last about two weeks, with predictable patterns of fund inflow: about 50% on the first day, about 20% during the middle stage, and about 30% on the last day. To optimize this pattern, you can offer price discounts to first-day investors to create positive initial momentum, while continuing communication and marketing during the middle of the sale, and increasing efforts in the final stage.

Post-sale work is equally important: delivering on product promises, encouraging long-term holding through mechanisms such as staking rewards, and maintaining transparent communication with regular updates to the community about product development and milestone achievements.

Success stories like AA, starting with a $600,000 presale in 2017, followed by raising $16 million in an ICO, now with a market cap of $2.6 billion, demonstrate the long-term potential of carefully executed token sales.

## Practical Considerations for Token Fundraising

### Legal and Regulatory Factors

The regulatory environment for token sales is constantly evolving, especially in Europe, where MiCA regulations provide a clearer framework for utility tokens. However, regulatory complexity still exists, especially for cross-border token sales.

For utility tokens, it's important to ensure they truly provide utility value to holders, not just as speculative instruments. For security tokens, you need to comply with strict securities regulations, including investor qualification screening, information disclosure, and ongoing reporting obligations.

KYC (Know Your Customer) requirements also vary depending on fundraising scale and investor type. Generally, for small investors (less than €1,000), lightweight KYC may be adopted; medium-scale investors (€1,000-10,000) need basic identity verification; and large investors (over €10,000) require a complete KYC process.

### Technical and Security Considerations

Token generation and sales involve complex technical implementation, and security is critical. Using audited smart contracts can greatly reduce risks, but you still need to pay attention to some key settings, such as whether to enable minting functionality (which might allow the creation of additional tokens, potentially diluting existing holders).

Token scanners and blockchain browsers will make your token transparent to the public, so ensuring technical implementation aligns with your promises to investors is crucial. Investors will check smart contracts for "honeypots" or other security vulnerabilities, so addressing these issues in advance is very important.

### Community Building and Management

Community is at the core of token sale success and a key driver for subsequent token value growth. Building a healthy community includes not just growth in numbers but also quality engagement.

Effective community management includes regular communication, transparent progress updates, educational content, and incentive mechanisms. Community members are not just investors but also early adopters of the product and brand ambassadors; their enthusiasm for the project can create a self-reinforcing growth cycle.

Beyond traditional social media channels, crypto-specific platforms like Discord and Telegram are usually the core venues for community building. Creating internal FAQs and training customer support teams to answer investor questions are also important components of community management.

## Best Practices for Token Fundraising

Based on successful case experiences, here are some best practices for token fundraising:
1. Prioritizing community building is the most fundamental strategy; establish an organic user base before launching token sales—these early supporters often become the most loyal investors.
2. Maintaining transparency is crucial for building trust; clearly communicating token purpose, fundraising goals, and fund allocation plans is the foundation for building investor confidence.
3. Ensuring product-market fit is key to avoiding failure; validate product demand before fundraising, avoiding raising funds without a clear direction.
4. Designing fair token economics requires balancing various interests, avoiding excessive concentration of token holdings, and ensuring the founding team's incentives align with long-term success.
5. Implementing reasonable token release mechanisms can prevent concentrated selling by early investors and team members, stabilizing token prices and encouraging long-term participation.
6. Collaborating with professional advisors can help navigate complex legal and technical issues; while this increases costs, it can avoid potentially expensive mistakes.

## Conclusion

Token fundraising provides entrepreneurs with an innovative financing path with unique advantages: a broader investor base, better liquidity, and alignment of incentive mechanisms. For projects at the right stage, especially those with strong communities in crypto and blockchain, it may be more attractive than traditional venture capital.

However, this is not a shortcut. Successful token fundraising requires careful planning, professional execution, and continuous effort. From legal compliance to community building to subsequent product development, each link needs to be taken seriously.

Ultimately, token fundraising is not just a way to raise funds, but to create an ecosystem with a shared vision, where the interests of users, investors, and founders are closely connected. When this alignment is achieved, it can create powerful growth momentum, driving the project toward success.

For founders considering token fundraising, it's advisable to first invest time in researching successful cases, building active communities, and ensuring your product and token design truly solve market needs. Only then can token fundraising become a powerful tool in your entrepreneurial journey, not just a short-term source of funds.
