# Securities Tokenization: The Possibilities, Pathways, and Long-term Impact of Bringing US Stocks On-chain

In the cryptocurrency market, certain narratives cycle through, reemerging in new forms across different market cycles. Securities Token Offerings (STOs) are one such narrative. This topic, which garnered significant attention a few years ago before gradually cooling off, has recently regained prominence following an announcement from Coinbase. This article, based on an in-depth conversation with Min Dao, founder of dForce, explores the prospects, challenges, and potential impacts of STOs, particularly the tokenization of US stocks.

## Background and Development of Coinbase Stock Tokenization

### Early Exploration and Attempts

Coinbase's exploration of stock tokenization didn't begin today. As early as 2020, Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong proposed this concept, but due to the extremely strict regulatory environment for the cryptocurrency industry at that time, coupled with Coinbase's subsequent lawsuit by the SEC, this idea never materialized. When Coinbase went public, there was debate within the community: as a cryptocurrency exchange, should it choose traditional listing or directly issue tokens on the blockchain?

### Shift in Regulatory Environment

In January this year, Base's head Jesse revisited this topic, considering deploying Coinbase tokens on the Base chain. Subsequently, in February, the SEC dropped its lawsuit against Coinbase, which was seen as a reflection of the new administration's friendly attitude toward the crypto industry. Following this, both Coinbase's CEO and CFO expressed their intention to issue stock tokens on Base. The timing of this choice is highly strategic, coinciding with a shift in the US political environment's attitude toward the crypto industry, including the convening of the Crypto Summit, which Coinbase's CEO attended. This change in regulatory attitude has provided more favorable ground for STO development.

### Backed Finance's Early Attempt

After Coinbase announced plans to restart its tokenization program, Switzerland's RWA service provider Backed Finance jumped ahead and issued a tokenized version of Coinbase stock on the Base chain. However, recent data shows that trading volume is low and there are serious de-pegging issues. This phenomenon reflects the many practical operational challenges still facing stock tokenization and indicates that not all stocks are suitable for tokenization.

## Value Proposition of Stock Tokenization

### Blockchain Applications in the Financial Sector

The concept of stock tokenization is not new; projects attempted to push in this direction as early as the 2017 bull market. It has regained attention in the current market cycle, primarily for several reasons:

First, while the cryptocurrency industry has been hindered by regulatory reasons, blockchain technology has made significant progress in traditional finance. Financial giants like JP Morgan have tokenized interbank settlement systems, greatly reducing the time and cost of interstate or international settlements. In fact, most traditional banking systems are based on decades-old technology, and the introduction of blockchain systems has significantly reduced cross-bank and cross-border settlement costs, greatly improving capital efficiency.

### From Regional to Global Markets

Second, stock tokenization can expand funding channels from regional markets to the global market. However, this also brings challenges, as traditional securities regulation follows territorial principles, while the crypto public chain market is borderless. This conflict has led to stock tokenization being likened to the "fall of the Berlin Wall," opening new channels for free world capital to flow into traditional stock markets. This characteristic has important implications for expanding corporate financing channels and improving capital market efficiency.

### Stock Empowerment: Beyond Traditional Shareholder Certificates

Coinbase's stock tokenization differs fundamentally from general stock tokenization. As an exchange, Coinbase's stock can be compared to Binance's BNB. BNB is essentially a successful case of token stock-ization, while Coinbase is a pure exchange stock. BNB far exceeds traditional stocks in terms of utility value, as it can be used to offset transaction fees, participate in airdrops, and more.

The greatest significance of Coinbase issuing stock tokens lies not just in putting stocks on the chain, but in "empowering" traditional stocks—this is a kind of "expansion of rights." For example, if Coinbase's stock is tokenized and placed on the Base chain, it could potentially be used for staking, node validation, or paying gas fees, transforming it from merely a shareholder certificate into an asset with practical utility.

### New Opportunities for Consumer-Oriented Enterprises

This concept can be extended to other companies, such as Disney and Netflix. Holders of Disney stock tokens might enjoy ticket discounts; Netflix subscription fees could be reduced based on the amount of stock a user stakes. Implementing these features is highly complex in traditional financial systems, but through tokenization, these companies can directly link shareholder rights with user experience, creating new business models and user incentive mechanisms.

For traditional industries like coal mining, stock tokenization may struggle to provide additional use value. Therefore, companies that have direct interaction with consumers, or those with high overlap with cryptocurrency user groups, may be more suitable for stock tokenization and could benefit more from it.

## US Stocks On-chain and DeFi Ecosystem Interaction

### Current State of DeFi Infrastructure

For DeFi infrastructure, upstream asset support currently focuses mainly on native cryptocurrency assets (such as ETH, BTC). Introducing stock tokenization will bring more asset types to DeFi protocols, increasing liquidity and application scenarios.

After the development from DeFi Summer to the present, DeFi's infrastructure has become quite sophisticated, with mature solutions in AMM form, order book form, and perpetual contract form. Various cross-chain bridges have also been built and tested by hacking incidents. This means that when new asset types like stocks are integrated, there's no need to rebuild the infrastructure; they can directly use existing systems.

### RWA and DeFi Integration Trends

Treasury bond tokenization has achieved significant results in the current cycle, with projects like Makerdao and Ondo placing treasury bonds on-chain. These Real World Assets (RWA) provide DeFi protocols with a large number of interest-bearing assets. Stock tokenization will continue this trend, injecting new vitality into the mature DeFi infrastructure.

From DeFi Summer until now, a large number of stablecoins and RWA assets have appeared in the DeFi ecosystem, providing important liquidity support for the entire ecosystem. If stock tokens successfully integrate, they can further enrich DeFi's asset classes, enhancing the resilience and attractiveness of the entire ecosystem.

### Comparison with Traditional Financial Infrastructure

Compared to traditional financial infrastructure, blockchain systems have obvious advantages in handling settlement and clearing. In traditional banking systems, even between different systems of the same bank, settlement and clearing face numerous problems, with cross-country settlements being even more complex. Blockchain systems greatly simplify this process through a unified ledger structure that keeps all states consistent. This is why stablecoins gained rapid adoption upon launch, as they effectively replaced the settlement function between traditional banking systems.

## Obstacles and Challenges to Stock Tokenization

### Issuer Credibility and Recognition

Despite promising prospects, stock tokenization still faces numerous challenges:

The credibility of the issuer is crucial. Like stablecoins, the issuer's reputation directly affects investor confidence. For example, if Coinbase's stock tokens were issued by major financial institutions like JP Morgan or Bank of America, rather than third-party institutions like Backed Finance, their credibility might be significantly higher.

### Redemption Mechanisms and De-pegging Risks

Investors need to be confident that the tokens they purchase can ultimately be exchanged for the underlying assets. If there aren't enough market makers supporting and allowing for the exchange of underlying tokens, serious de-pegging issues may arise. Stablecoins maintain their peg to the dollar because a large number of minters and market makers can redeem them for dollars through channels like Coinbase and Tether. If the redemption channels for stock tokens aren't smooth, it becomes difficult to maintain price consistency with the underlying stocks.

### Insufficient Liquidity Issues

If there aren't enough market makers, liquidity is weak, or redemption channels aren't smooth, token prices may significantly deviate from underlying stock prices. Sufficient liquidity is a key factor in maintaining price stability, requiring issuers to attract enough market makers to participate.

### Barriers to Token Rights Expansion

Only the stock-issuing company itself can truly grant additional rights to tokens; third parties cannot provide these value-added services. For example, Coinbase can allow its stock tokens to be staked or pay gas fees on the Base chain, but third-party issuers like Backed Finance cannot provide such functionality. This creates an important limitation in token rights expansion.

### Uncertainty in Market Demand

Cryptocurrency investors can typically buy US stocks directly through offshore accounts, so the demand for cryptocurrency users to purchase US stocks as a pure capital pool may not meet expectations. This is one reason why various attempts in the past few years have not achieved significant success. If stock tokenization only provides the same functionality as traditional channels without additional value propositions, it may struggle to attract a sufficient user base.

### Regulatory Challenges and Compliance Requirements

Another significant obstacle facing stock tokenization is regulatory challenges. If following traditional securities regulations, stock tokens belong to security tokens and may only allow users who have passed KYC (Know Your Customer) certification to purchase. This would greatly limit their audience, making them more like competing with traditional brokerage channels for existing markets rather than opening up incremental markets.

## Market Prospects and Development Path for STOs

### Changes in Market Structure

Compared to early markets, the current cryptocurrency ecosystem has undergone significant changes. With the launch of Bitcoin ETFs and Ethereum ETFs, traditional financial funds have heavily entered the crypto space for allocation. At the same time, stablecoins have accumulated about $230-240 billion in minting volume, but only a few tens of billions of dollars actually remain in the DeFi ecosystem; most users simply hold stablecoins as a substitute for US dollars.

### Diversification of User Groups

This change in market structure means that a large portion of current crypto market users come from traditional financial backgrounds and have clear demand for stock-like assets. Past crypto market users mostly followed the "long crypto, short the world" ideology, with limited interest in traditional assets. Now, with the influx of more non-crypto-native users, the market demand for traditional assets has notably increased.

### Development Expectations for the Next Three to Five Years

Overall, US stock tokenization is still in its early stages, similar to DeFi in 2017-2018, and has not yet reached its true explosion period. In the next three to five years, as long as a compliant framework can be established allowing these assets to be issued on-chain, the US stock tokenization market is expected to form a certain volume. The key lies in solving the problem of compliant issuance; once the regulatory framework is clear, the market could develop rapidly.

### Comparison with Stablecoin Development Paths

US stock tokenization may follow a development path similar to stablecoins. It may face challenges in the initial stage, but once market trust and liquidity networks are established, growth could accelerate. The success of stablecoins demonstrates that there is a huge market space for tokenization of real-world assets, and stocks, as another important category of traditional assets, have the same tokenization potential.

## Beneficiaries of the STO Wave and Industry Structure

### Core Advantages of Asset Issuers

Looking at the development history of stablecoins, the biggest beneficiaries of US stock tokenization are likely to be asset issuers. Here, asset issuers refers not to companies like Tesla or Disney themselves, but to the institutions that tokenize these companies' stocks and issue them on-chain.

For stock tokens and stablecoins, the most critical factor is the network effect of liquidity. Once a Matthew effect forms, it becomes difficult to replace. If an institution can tokenize high-volume indexes like QQQ or the NASDAQ index and place them on-chain, forming a liquidity network effect similar to USDT, then these issuers will become the biggest winners.

### Diversification of Business Models

These issuers can perform operations like margin financing at the underlying level, charging various fees and establishing diversified business models. Moreover, just like in the ETF market, early movers typically occupy a larger market share, forming lasting advantages. After stock tokenization, more financial products and services may derive, creating new revenue sources for issuers.

### Blockchain Selection Strategy

Regarding the choice of blockchain for asset issuance, the best strategy might be to emulate USDT by issuing on all mainstream chains. Part of USDT's success is due to its existence when there were no other alternatives in the market, being accepted by all exchanges and forming a strong network effect. For stock tokens, they should be issued wherever there is demand, rather than being limited to a specific blockchain.

Whether it's Ethereum, Solana, or Base, each blockchain platform has its advantages and user communities. What truly matters is the scale and depth of market demand, not the technical characteristics of the blockchain itself. Similar to USDT's multi-chain deployment, stock tokenization may adopt a similar multi-chain strategy to maximize coverage of potential users.

## Policy Environment and Political Influence in the Crypto Industry

### Changes in US Policy Environment

Beyond the topic of STOs, the current policy environment of the cryptocurrency industry is also worth noting. Recently, there have been many changes at the US policy level, including Trump's issuance of a strategic reserve executive order for Bitcoin and the Trump family's launch of the World Liberty project to purchase various crypto assets.

From a policy perspective, many favorable factors have indeed emerged, with the degree and speed of favorability exceeding expectations. Viewing Bitcoin as a strategic reserve asset for the US, this positioning has reached a high level in terms of both execution speed and definitional height.

### Complex Impact of Political Factors

However, there are also concerning factors. Trump's issuance of tokens and a series of operations by World Liberty Finance have made some industry insiders uneasy. The cryptocurrency industry has long prided itself on its anti-fragility and independence from government, but now seems to be hijacked by specific political forces. This excessive connection with political figures may bring risks, especially considering the possibility of political transitions.

### Executive Orders and Legal Framework

Most of Trump's measures have been implemented through Executive Orders (EOs), which a new administration could easily repeal. Therefore, the key for the next four years is to see how many policies can be formally embedded in the US legislative framework, becoming laws rather than just remaining at the executive order level. This is crucial for the long-term development of US stock tokenization and the entire crypto industry.

### Progress and Setbacks in State-Level Legislation

It's worth noting that despite favorable policies at the federal level, many states' Bitcoin reserve plans have failed to pass. Relevant legislation in about four or five states has not successfully passed, reflecting to some extent the complexity at the policy implementation level. This difference between federal and state-level policies is also one of the challenges facing the cryptocurrency industry.

## Conclusion

US stock tokenization represents an important direction for the integration of traditional finance and blockchain technology. Despite challenges, with changes in market structure and improvements in technological infrastructure, this field is expected to make substantial progress in the coming years. For investors and industry participants, understanding the driving factors of this trend, potential obstacles, and possible beneficiaries will help better grasp future development opportunities.

At the same time, we also need to pay attention to changes in the policy environment of the cryptocurrency industry, especially the regulatory attitudes of major markets like the US. The sustainability of policies and the stability of the legal framework will largely determine the speed and depth of integration between cryptocurrencies and traditional finance.
