# Beyond the Dollar: Stablecoins and the Battle for Financial Influence in Asia

Stablecoins, serving as bridges between the blockchain world and traditional finance, are redefining the global financial order. This isn't merely a technological innovation but a transformation reshaping international financial power structures. Against the backdrop of digital dollarization, China's digital yuan push, and central banks considering CBDCs, stablecoins have evolved from simple cryptocurrency tools into extensions of national financial sovereignty and competitive arenas. Based on insights from Jeff Wen, Vice Chairman of Taiwan Financial Technology Association, this article dissects the geopolitical implications of stablecoins and Taiwan's opportunities and challenges in this global competition involving digital sovereignty.

## The Nature and Current State of Stablecoins

### The Rise and Evolution of USD Stablecoins

Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies pegged to fiat currencies, designed to combine the technological advantages of cryptocurrencies with the value stability of fiat currencies. They solve the high volatility problem of traditional cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, enabling blockchain technology to truly integrate into daily financial activities. Currently, the most prominent stablecoins are USDT (Tether) and USDC (USD Coin), which together account for over 95% of the entire stablecoin market (approximately $230-240 billion), forming a de facto "duopoly."

**USDT's Taiwan Origins and the Catalyst of Gray Industries**

USDT has little-known deep connections with Taiwan. According to industry insiders, its origin can be traced to an office in Banqiao, Taiwan. This choice wasn't coincidental but a carefully considered decision. Taiwan, as a global IT talent hub, possesses excellent engineering resources; simultaneously, it serves as an important operational base for "gray industries" in the Asia-Pacific region (such as online gambling, offshore finance, and other businesses not taxed locally).

USDT was created specifically to serve industries requiring efficient, low-transparency cross-border payments. At the time, market participants in these "unbanked" regions faced exclusion from traditional financial systems and urgently needed a tool for quick, inexpensive, and relatively anonymous value transfers. USDT, with its borderless, instant, and relatively anonymous features, perfectly filled this need gap.

**USDT vs. USDC: The Financial Competition Between "Straw Sandals" and "Leather Shoes"**

In contrast to USDT's "grassroots beginning," USDC is issued by Circle, backed by Wall Street institutions like Goldman Sachs, and has been committed to regulatory compliance since its inception. Using an illustrative metaphor, USDT is the "straw sandal-wearing" grassroots representative serving emerging markets and gray industries, while USDC is the "leather shoe-wearing" Western representative, catering to mainstream financial institutions and clients with high compliance requirements.

These two stablecoins demonstrate distinctly different paths in their business philosophies and financial performance:

- **USDT**: Follows a "light asset" strategy with astonishing net profits—approximately $13 billion in 2023 with a team of only about 200 people, possibly the highest per capita net profit contribution among global tech companies. Its business model centers on "regulatory arbitrage"—providing financial services without bearing the regulatory costs and compliance obligations of traditional financial institutions.

- **USDC**: Adopts a "heavy asset" strategy which, while more compliant, incurs high operational costs with substantial investments in personnel and infrastructure. Its Manhattan headquarters is located in the expensive World Trade Financial Center, and the company's CEO earns an annual salary of tens of millions of dollars, reflecting an operational style that embodies the luxury and standards of traditional finance.

These starkly different operational models reflect two distinct stablecoin philosophies: one prioritizing flexibility and maximum profit, the other prioritizing compliance and willing to sacrifice certain profits for regulatory approval and institutional trust.

### USDT's Regulatory Arbitrage and Future Transformation

**The Golden Age of Regulatory Arbitrage**

A key factor in USDT's success is what's known as "regulatory arbitrage"—providing essentially financial services (such as asset custody and value exchange) without bearing the regulatory costs and compliance requirements of traditional financial institutions. In the early development stage of the cryptocurrency industry, this model allowed Tether to achieve extremely high profit margins with very low operational costs.

USDT's "regulatory arbitrage" has faced multiple challenges. In 2021, Tether was investigated by the New York Attorney General and fined $18.5 million for false statements about its asset reserves. Even earlier, USDT's reserve integrity had been questioned multiple times, with various "de-pegging" crises (price deviations from $1) occurring periodically. These crises often required substantial financial support to overcome. Industry speculation suggests that funds from Taiwan's local "gray industries" may have provided crucial backing.

**From Confrontation to Symbiosis**

However, with the Trump administration's more friendly attitude toward cryptocurrencies and the entire industry moving toward institutionalization and compliance, this "regulatory arbitrage" model is transforming. Tether's strategy is shifting from "regulatory avoidance" to "regulatory participation."

In early 2024, Tether's Italian CEO set foot on US soil for the first time and engaged with regulatory authorities, marking a significant shift in their relationship. For Tether executives who once viewed US territory as a "restricted zone," this step signifies that the company has reached some level of understanding with the US Department of Commerce.

Once the US stablecoin bill passes, the operational model of stablecoins will more closely resemble traditional finance:

1. **Increased Compliance Costs**: Requirements to pay regulatory fees, complete more stringent audits, and maintain higher capital reserves will significantly increase operational costs.

2. **Reduced Profit Margins**: Due to increased costs, the net profits of stablecoins like USDT may decrease to half or less of their current levels, although the overall market size will continue to expand.

3. **Expanded User Base**: Higher compliance standards will attract more institutional investors and corporate users, further expanding the application range of stablecoins.

4. **Industry Consolidation**: Smaller stablecoin providers may be unable to bear the increased compliance costs, leading to further market concentration among major players.

This transformation reflects the cryptocurrency industry's transition from the "early innovation phase" to the "institutionalization middle phase." Stablecoins, as bridges connecting traditional finance and the blockchain world, are inevitably at the forefront of this transition.

## The Geopolitical Significance of Stablecoins

### Digital Extension of US Dollar Power and Financial Hegemony Reconstruction

The strategic significance of stablecoins far exceeds the realm of financial innovation or technological advancement; they have essentially become tools for extending US dollar hegemony in the digital era. Through USD stablecoins like USDT and USDC, the United States has seamlessly extended its financial influence into the blockchain world, achieving a power transition from physical to digital dollars.

**Maintaining the Global Dollar Cycle**

USD stablecoins enable the US to maintain and strengthen the global financial cycle mechanism it has established over decades:

1. **US Consumes, World Produces**: The US consumes by issuing bonds and dollars, while other countries (especially Asian manufacturing nations) provide goods and services to the US.

2. **Foreign Reserves in Dollar Assets**: Central banks and institutional investors from various countries reinvest their earned dollars in US Treasury bonds and other dollar assets. For example, about 92% of Taiwan's foreign exchange reserves (over $500 billion) are in US Treasury bonds, reflecting deep dependence on the dollar system.

3. **Continuous Dollar Penetration**: The dollar, as a global settlement currency, continues to penetrate into economic systems worldwide, strengthening US financial influence.

Stablecoins add a new digital dimension to this cycle, allowing the dollar to penetrate more deeply into previously hard-to-reach markets and sectors, especially in the cryptocurrency economies of developing countries.

**The Stablecoin Bill and MAGA Strategy**

Within the Trump administration's "America First" strategic framework, the stablecoin bill holds a central position. Its importance is comparable to tariff policies; in fact, the stablecoin bill and tariff policies are complementary aspects of the same strategy:

- **Tariff Policy**: Reshapes physical economic trade relationships, strengthening US manufacturing
- **Stablecoin Bill**: Restructures the financial foundation of the digital economy, ensuring dollar dominance in the crypto world

The stablecoin bill is expected to be significant legislation in Trump's new term, with the core goal of incorporating USD stablecoins into the regulatory framework while preventing other countries from challenging US digital dominance. This bill will allow US financial institutions to participate more deeply in the stablecoin market while maintaining regulatory control, achieving a balance between "compliance and innovation."

**The Fusion of Digital Power and National Security**

Stablecoins have evolved from purely financial tools into national security issues. Controlling the global stablecoin market means controlling global digital value flows, which is crucial for the US to maintain its global leadership position. USDT and USDC together control 95% of the stablecoin market, giving the US unprecedented influence over global digital asset flows.

### The Global Stablecoin War: A New Battlefield in US-China Digital Currency Competition

**China's Parallel Path: Digital Yuan Ambitions and Setbacks**

China's digital yuan (DCEP, Digital Currency Electronic Payment) represents a development path distinctly different from USD stablecoins. As the first central bank digital currency (CBDC) of a major economy, DCEP embodies China's emphasis on financial sovereignty and intent to challenge the dollar system.

China has established a multi-level digital financial infrastructure attempting to achieve a certain degree of "de-dependence" from the dollar system:

1. **CIPS System**: China's Cross-Border Inter-Bank Payment System, designed to provide an alternative international payment and settlement channel to SWIFT.

2. **EMBREACH Project**: Promoting digital yuan adoption in countries along the "Belt and Road" initiative.

3. **Regional Settlement Network**: Attempting to establish RMB-based settlement networks in Southeast Asia, ASEAN countries, and the Middle East.

These systems have technically achieved connectivity, but the biggest challenge is not technical feasibility but market acceptance—"who is willing to use it?" Apart from the forced use by Chinese enterprises in overseas engineering projects like "Belt and Road" construction, cases of voluntary adoption of the digital yuan for international settlement remain limited.

**The 2018-2019 Cryptocurrency Ban: A Lost Opportunity**

China's decision to ban Bitcoin and other cryptocurrency transactions through red-headed documents in 2018-2019 may, from a strategic perspective, have been a major misstep. This policy forced domestic cryptocurrency exchanges like Binance to move overseas, causing China to lose the opportunity to nurture and control its native cryptocurrency ecosystem.

If China had adopted a regulatory rather than prohibitive strategy at that time, forcing these exchanges to provide CNY (RMB) trading pairs, the outcome might have been dramatically different:

- RMB stablecoins might have already formed a force to counter USD stablecoins
- China might have controlled important nodes in global digital asset trading
- RMB internationalization might have achieved some breakthrough through crypto channels

Instead, this ban effectively pushed China's crypto talent and capital overseas, objectively strengthening the dollar's dominance in the crypto world—a "gift to the United States."

**The Regionalization Trend in Stablecoin Competition**

Today, the first phase of global stablecoin competition has ended, with USD stablecoins dominating the market with overwhelming advantages. Even BUSD, the native stablecoin of Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has been unable to shake the position of USDT and USDC.

Stablecoin competition is entering a second phase—the contest for regional stablecoins:

1. **National Fiat Stablecoins**: Financially developed countries like the G7 and G20 are beginning to consider issuing stablecoin versions of their national fiat currencies to reduce friction costs when trading with USD stablecoins.

2. **Seamless On-chain to Off-chain Conversion**: Reducing the costs of "on-ramp" and "off-ramp" (getting on and off the blockchain), which currently stands at 0.5% to 1% in Taiwan, already among the lowest globally.

3. **Regional Financial Sovereignty**: Stablecoins have become extensions of digital financial sovereignty; countries issuing their own stablecoins are motivated not only by convenience but also by maintaining financial autonomy.

This phase of competition will be more complex, no longer a simple US dollar vs. RMB binary opposition, but a multipolar regional competition where each region tries to secure more autonomy within the dollar-dominated digital financial system.

## Taiwan's Stablecoin Opportunities and Challenges: A Test of Digital Financial Sovereignty

### Why Taiwan Needs to Issue New Taiwan Dollar Stablecoins

Against the backdrop of intensifying global stablecoin competition, issuing New Taiwan Dollar stablecoins is no longer just an option for financial innovation but a strategic necessity concerning digital economic sovereignty. Its key significance is reflected in several aspects:

**1. Reducing Systemic Costs of Cross-border Transactions**

Currently, the conversion between cryptocurrencies and fiat currencies (commonly known as "on-ramping" and "off-ramping") requires paying substantial fees. While this cost in Taiwan has reached the lowest level globally, it still amounts to 0.5% to 1%.

For a company engaged in large international settlements:
- A $500 million transaction could incur on-ramping and off-ramping costs of $2.5 to $5 million
- With NTD stablecoins, businesses could settle directly in Taiwan dollars on-chain, avoiding these costs

This "friction cost" not only affects corporate profits but also reduces Taiwan's competitiveness in the global financial network.

**2. Serving the Needs of Taiwan's Global Manufacturing Leaders**

Taiwan, as one of the global "Big Five" in electronic manufacturing, is home to global operations giants like Foxconn. These companies face practical challenges including:

- **Emerging Market Payment Dilemmas**: Payments from emerging markets like Brazil are increasingly made in stablecoins, and traditional Taiwanese banks lack the capacity to receive such payments.
- **International Supply Chain Management**: Need to pay suppliers, partners, and employees globally, with traditional cross-border payment systems being time-consuming and costly.
- **Capital Efficiency Management**: Large enterprises need effective management of idle funds, and on-chain financial tools can provide greater flexibility and returns.

If Taiwanese banks cannot provide corresponding services to these flagship enterprises, the companies may be forced to seek assistance from overseas financial institutions, resulting in the loss of high-value clients from the Taiwanese financial sector.

**3. Maintaining the International Competitiveness of the Financial System**

Financial services are a fiercely competitive global market. If Taiwan's banking industry cannot provide cryptocurrency custody and stablecoin services, it will face competitive challenges on multiple fronts:

- Multinational banks may capture Taiwan's large corporate clients
- International fintech companies may bypass local Taiwanese banks to provide advanced digital financial services
- Regional financial centers like Hong Kong and Singapore may strengthen their leading positions in digital assets

**4. Maintaining Financial Sovereignty in the Digital Era**

In the digital asset era, the definition of financial sovereignty is expanding to include not only traditional currency issuance rights but also discourse power in the digital asset ecosystem. If Taiwan's currency has no corresponding representation in the blockchain world, it will face:

- Marginalization in the digital financial landscape
- Excessive dependence on international stablecoin providers
- Lack of voice in future digital economy rule-making

### Taiwan's Banking Industry Strategic Transformation: From Observation to Action

**Cryptocurrency Custody: The First Step in Digital Asset Strategy**

Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission allowed banks to pilot cryptocurrency custody services in late 2023, with five banks currently participating in this program. Behind this policy shift is a recognition of global digital asset trends and a response to the needs of domestic enterprises.

However, these services are not aimed at all customers but primarily at large corporate clients. According to industry sources, banks have set high thresholds for these services, with clients having assets below NT$100 billion potentially finding it difficult to access them.

This is just the first step. Taiwan's banking industry also needs to prepare to provide on-chain Taiwan dollar (New Taiwan Dollar stablecoin) services to meet the needs of enterprises for on-chain Taiwan dollar-denominated transactions, salary payments, and other requirements, avoiding the costs of repeated on-ramping and off-ramping. For example, after receiving USD stablecoins, if a company needs to pay salaries in Taiwan dollars, it currently must first exchange the stablecoins for fiat currency, a process that is both time-consuming and costly.

## Financial Innovation Driven by Stablecoins: The Rise of RWA

### Real World Assets On-chain

The success of stablecoins is leading to another wave of financial innovation: the tokenization of real-world assets (RWA). RWA refers to the transformation of traditional financial assets such as bonds, stocks, and funds into tokens on the blockchain.

After stablecoins connected the Web2 and Web3 financial worlds, enterprises and institutions found themselves holding large amounts of funds on-chain and began seeking investment channels for these funds. This has promoted the migration of traditional financial assets to blockchain, forming the RWA trend.

Global asset management giants like BlackRock are heavily investing in the RWA space, tokenizing US Treasury Bills and other instruments to launch products like BUIDL. These tokenized products have several significant advantages over traditional financial products:

1. **Instant Settlement**: Traditional financial products like Money Market Funds typically take T+3 to T+7 days to complete transactions, while tokenized products can achieve second-level settlement. For enterprises managing large amounts of funds, time is money, and each second of early settlement can bring additional income.

2. **Continuous Interest Accrual**: Traditional financial products typically calculate interest daily or monthly, while tokenized products can calculate interest hourly or even by the second. Programmatic smart contracts make this fine-grained interest calculation possible, providing more precise fund appreciation.

3. **Cross-platform Utilization**: Traditional financial products can often only be used at specific institutions; for example, a fund purchased at Yuanta cannot be redeemed at Fubon Bank. Tokenized assets, however, can be used across multiple platforms without being restricted to a single institution, greatly enhancing asset liquidity.

4. **Reduced Intermediary Costs**: Fewer intermediaries mean lower transaction friction and costs. Traditional financial products often involve multiple intermediary institutions, each charging fees, while tokenized products achieve more direct transactions through smart contracts, reducing overall costs.

For example, a dollar short-term debt token like BUIDL allows users to invest a minimum of $5 million and earn returns on US short-term Treasury bonds while maintaining extremely high liquidity. These tokens can also be used as collateral on cryptocurrency exchanges like Binance, OKX, or Coinbase, further expanding their utility.

### Stablecoins as the "WhatsApp Moment"

Just as WhatsApp disrupted the traditional telecommunications industry by making information transmission borderless, instant, and free, stablecoins are bringing a similar revolution to value transfer. This analogy comes from a16z's Chris Dixon, who compared the impact of stablecoins to a "WhatsApp moment."

In the information domain, Web2 (like WhatsApp) achieved borderless, instant, and free message transmission. In the financial domain, stablecoins are achieving similar breakthroughs for value transfer:

- **Borderless**: Stablecoins can easily cross geographical and political boundaries
- **Instant**: Transaction confirmation times have shrunk from days in traditional banking to seconds
- **Nearly Free**: Through new generation blockchain technologies like Layer 2, Arbitrum, or Solana, the cost of cross-border fund transfers has decreased to about $0.01

In comparison, the traditional international remittance system SWIFT has an average fee of around $44, usually takes 3-12 days for processing, and often needs to pass through 5-6 intermediary banks. This efficiency gap gives stablecoins revolutionary significance for cross-border payments.

## Digital Identity and Future Challenges

### The Integration of Digital Identity and Stablecoins

In the future, Taiwan's digital identity program and digital currency will be closely integrated. The government-issued digital ID will integrate with digital wallets, similar to the currently common "Sign in with Google" or "Sign in with Apple" functions on websites, except it will become "Sign in with Republic of China ID."

This integration will simplify KYC (Know Your Customer) procedures, allowing users to easily authenticate and use digital financial services. Users will need only one identity verification to use their digital identity across multiple platforms and services, greatly improving convenience.

A key question facing this integration is: Can Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) coexist with private stablecoins? Current trends suggest the market seems to favor regulated stablecoins issued by private institutions rather than CBDCs fully controlled by governments. Especially against the backdrop of the Trump administration's clear statement that it will not promote CBDCs, the position of USD stablecoins may be further consolidated.

### Balancing Privacy and Regulation

While stablecoins provide convenient value transfer, they are not completely anonymous. Mainstream stablecoins like USDT retain remote freezing capabilities, allowing transaction tracing and fund freezing when necessary. This indicates that, contrary to common perception, using stablecoins for money laundering or illegal activities actually carries high risks.

With the introduction of global stablecoin bills, information sharing between countries will strengthen, making regulation in the digital asset world more stringent. When identities are bound to digital wallets, all transaction behaviors will become traceable, bringing new privacy challenges.

In the future, users may need to manage multiple digital identities and wallets to balance convenience and privacy needs. For example, a "primary wallet" for formal financial activities, a "secondary wallet" for daily consumption, and possibly even an "anonymous wallet" for specific privacy needs. This complexity may require assistance from tools like AI to manage different digital identities.

## Conclusion

Stablecoins are redefining the global financial landscape, from the digital extension of dollar hegemony to competition among countries issuing their own stablecoins. For Taiwan, timely deployment of New Taiwan Dollar stablecoins is not just about financial innovation but also concerns digital sovereignty and international competitiveness.

In this transformation, Taiwan's financial institutions need to adapt quickly to provide services that meet the demands of the digital era for businesses and individuals. At the same time, policymakers need to balance innovation, regulation, and privacy protection to find an appropriate position for Taiwan in the global digital financial landscape.

With the development of RWA and digital identity, we are entering a new era where value transfer is borderless, instant, and low-cost. In this era, stablecoins are not just financial tools but extensions of a nation's digital influence.
