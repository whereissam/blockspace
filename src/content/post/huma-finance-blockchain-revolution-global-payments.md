## The $4 Trillion Problem: The Truth About Global Payment Systems

Do you know how much money is sitting idle due to inefficiencies in payment systems globally?

**$4 trillion.**

That's right, $4,000,000,000,000. This equals Japan's entire annual GDP. But why is so much money sitting idle, and what's wrong with our current global payment infrastructure?

![gdp chart](https://i.imgur.com/6SdtFWp.png)

### The Pre-Funding Problem: Money Pools Around the World

The first major issue is the massive pre-funding requirement. Payment companies like Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, or cross-border remittance services need to set up "money pools" around the world. These pools ensure that when you consume abroad or transfer money to another country, local merchants or recipients can quickly receive funds in their local currency.

These companies typically need to pre-deposit reserves equaling 20-25% of a month's transaction volume in each market they serve. With over 100 countries worldwide, each needing such money pools, massive amounts of capital become locked and unproductive.

### The SWIFT System: Slow, Expensive, and Outdated

Beyond the pre-funding problem, our current international transfer system, dominated by SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication), is plagued with issues:

1. **High Costs**: SWIFT transfers often involve multiple intermediary banks, each taking their cut. A typical international transfer can cost anywhere from $20-50 in explicit fees. But there are also hidden costs in the form of unfavorable exchange rates, sometimes marking up the true cost by an additional 3-7%.
2. **Snail-Paced Settlements**: Despite our digital world, SWIFT transfers typically take 3-5 business days to complete. This timeline extends even further when transfers involve less common currency pairs or developing nations.
3. **Opaque Process**: Once you initiate a transfer, tracking becomes nearly impossible. Your money enters a black box, moving through multiple banks with little visibility until it (hopefully) arrives at its destination.
4. **Banking Hours Limitation**: SWIFT operates on banking hours. Transfers initiated on Friday evenings or weekends won't be processed until the next business day, adding further delays to an already slow process.
5. **Extensive Documentation**: International transfers often require extensive documentation and compliance checks, creating friction and further delaying the process.

### The Ripple Effects on Global Commerce

This inefficient system creates cascading effects throughout the global economy:

- **Working Capital Constraints**: Businesses must maintain higher cash reserves to compensate for payment delays, restricting their ability to invest in growth.
- **Cash Flow Problems for Small Businesses**: While large corporations can absorb payment delays, small businesses often struggle with cash flow issues when payments take days to arrive.
- **Limited Access to Global Markets**: High fees and complex processes effectively lock many small businesses out of international trade opportunities.
- **Currency Risk Exposure**: Longer settlement times mean greater exposure to currency fluctuations, creating additional risk and cost.

**The question is, in the blockchain era, do we really need to continue with this outdated system?**

Traditional cross-border payment infrastructure was designed in the 1970s, long before the internet, let alone blockchain. The pre-funding model not only locks up vast amounts of capital but combines with the SWIFT system's limitations to create a perfect storm of inefficiency.

Richard saw this dual problem, trapped capital and slow, expensive transfers and believed blockchain technology offered a comprehensive solution that could transform global finance. By leveraging blockchain's inherent cross-border capabilities and stablecoins' value stability, Huma is addressing both sides of this $4 trillion problem.

## How Huma Makes Money Move: A Deep Dive into the Operating Mechanism

![huma structure chart](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GY3ST8YbYAAXXs7?format=jpg&name=orig)

Want to know how Huma's magic works behind the scenes? The brilliance of Huma's system lies in how it reimagines cross-border payments using blockchain technology while solving real business problems. Let's break down the intricate machinery that powers this financial innovation.

### The Traditional System's Bottlenecks

To appreciate Huma's solution, we first need to understand exactly why traditional cross-border payments are so inefficient.

When a payment company like TransferWise (now Wise) or Western Union needs to process transfers across different countries, they face a fundamental challenge: they must maintain pre-funded accounts in each target market. For example, to facilitate transfers to Argentina, they need Argentine pesos already sitting in local banks.

This creates severe problems. First, there's massive capital immobilization where billions in funding sits idle in these pre-funded accounts. Second, geographic coverage becomes limited because each new country requires establishing new banking relationships and regulatory approvals. Third, liquidity management grows incredibly complex as companies must constantly predict and balance liquidity across dozens of currencies and markets.

The standard industry practice is to maintain prefunding equal to roughly 20-25% of monthly transaction volume in each market. With hundreds of markets globally, this translates to a staggering $4 trillion in trapped capital that could otherwise be deployed productively in the economy.

### Huma's B2B Solution: Reimagining the Infrastructure

Huma isn't targeting individual consumers directly. Instead, they partner with licensed payment institutions, providing them with the infrastructure to make these transfers more efficient. This B2B approach has allowed them to process $3.7 billion in transaction volume while staying relatively unknown to the general public.

Here's how the system works in detail:

#### Step 1: Payment Request Reception and Risk Assessment

When a payment service provider receives a customer's request to send money internationally, they turn to Huma. But before processing anything, Huma has already conducted rigorous due diligence.

This includes institutional partner vetting where only licensed financial institutions that meet strict regulatory requirements can join the network. Huma performs thorough balance sheet analysis on each partner's financial health. Sophisticated algorithms track historical transaction patterns to predict liquidity needs and detect anomalies. Partners may also be required to maintain certain collateral ratios based on their risk profile to ensure system security.

#### Step 2: Transaction Initialization and Custodial Protection

Once a transfer is initiated (for example, $1 million from Hong Kong to Argentina), the payment company places the $1 million in a monitored custodial account. This account is often held with a regulated third-party custodian, not directly controlled by either the payment company or Huma.

Smart contracts automatically verify the funds are properly secured before proceeding. These funds serve as collateral and can only be released under specific conditions defined in the smart contract, creating a secure environment for the transaction to proceed.

#### Step 3: Stablecoin Bridge Deployment

This is where blockchain technology transforms the process. Huma utilizes USDC on Solana for maximum speed and minimum cost, with transaction fees of fractions of a cent. Equivalent USDC tokens ($1 million worth) are immediately sent to Huma's partner institution in Argentina.

The transaction is confirmed within seconds on the Solana blockchain. Unlike traditional SWIFT messages that only contain instructions, this transfer represents actual value movement. The entire transaction is recorded on a public blockchain, providing unprecedented transparency that's impossible in traditional banking.

#### Step 4: Local Currency Settlement and Last-Mile Delivery

Upon receiving the USDC, the Argentine partner institution immediately begins the process of local currency conversion. They have pre-established relationships with local banks and payment networks, allowing them to distribute Argentine pesos to the final recipient via the appropriate local payment rail, whether that's bank transfer, mobile money, or cash pickup.

This settlement happens much faster than traditional methods, often same-day versus the 3-7 days typically required through correspondent banking relationships. The recipient gets their money quickly, while the entire process happens seamlessly behind the scenes.

#### Step 5: Fund Return and Fee Structure

Once the international leg is complete, the payment company in Hong Kong is notified that settlement has occurred. The smart contract authorizes the release of the original $1 million from the custodial account back to Huma.

Huma charges a daily fee (between 0.06% and 0.1%) for the period the funds were utilized. This translates to an annualized yield of 15-20%, but the actual fee is much lower since most transactions complete in 1-6 days. The payment company can build this cost into their customer pricing, still offering more competitive rates than traditional methods.

Security is paramount in financial infrastructure, so Huma has implemented rigorous smart contract protection. Their core protocols use immutable logic that cannot be altered even by administrators once deployed. They employ formal verification using mathematical proofs that code behaves exactly as intended. Critical operations require multi-signature approval from independent authorities. Major changes must pass through time-delay periods before implementation. They also run bug bounty programs where external security researchers are incentivized to find vulnerabilities.

## Stablecoins: The Key to Success

Cryptocurrency enthusiasts might ask, why not use Bitcoin or Ethereum directly for cross-border payments?

The simple answer is volatility.

Imagine if you pay $1 million using Bitcoin, and during the transaction, Bitcoin's price drops 5%. This means the recipient actually receives $50,000 less. This is unacceptable for commercial payments.

There are industry precedents. Ripple tried using XRP for cross-border payments, and despite good marketing, actual business volume was minimal. The reason was that XRP's price instability made it unsuitable for real payments, creating many problems for themselves instead.

In contrast, stablecoins (like USDC) have their value pegged to the dollar, providing necessary stability while retaining blockchain's speed advantages. This is precisely why Huma chose to use stablecoins.

Huma currently operates primarily on the Solana blockchain using USDC stablecoins, but its technical architecture supports multi-chain deployment. In the future, with the development of central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), Huma also plans to expand support for more stable assets.

## Redefining Success: Farewell to Vanity Metrics

In measuring success, the Huma team has a distinctive perspective. In the cryptocurrency industry, Total Value Locked (TVL) is typically viewed as an important indicator. But Richard strongly criticizes this.

He believes TVL is a vanity metric that has caused tremendous damage to cryptocurrencies, a destructiveness second only to human greed. Why? Because TVL encourages projects to attract large amounts of funds that aren't effectively used, creating waste.

Instead, he proposes new measurement standards. Total Effective Capital measures the amount of funds actually deployed and used, while Transaction Volume directly reflects the actual economic activity created by the platform.

Richard emphasizes, how much money is actually being put to use? This is the truly important question.

Huma's distinction lies in not pursuing high TVL that is merely for show, but ensuring every dollar on the platform is effectively utilized. This is also why you might not see Huma when checking DeFi rankings, as their fund pools typically maintain low levels of idle funds.
